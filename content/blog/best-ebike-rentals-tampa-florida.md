There are two ways to get on an electric bike in Tampa today. You can unlock a shared e-bike from
Lime or Spin with your phone, or you can rent one from a bike shop. They look interchangeable, and
for a lot of trips they are. But they come with very different rules, and the biggest difference
catches visitors out every day: **the app bikes are not allowed on the Tampa Riverwalk, Bayshore
Boulevard or 7th Avenue in Ybor City** - the three places most people want to ride.

This guide explains how each option works, where each one can and cannot go, and which to pick for
the trip you have in mind. It is not a ranking of shops. For that, see our
[10 best e-bike rentals in Tampa Bay & St. Pete](/blog/best-ebike-rentals-in-tampa-bay-st-pete-florida/),
or browse [every Tampa rental shop](/cities/tampa/).

## First: Tampa no longer has a docked bike share

If you remember the blue Coast Bike Share bikes, they are gone. HOPR, which ran Coast Bike Share,
says it has closed all of its sites in Tampa and no longer operates in Florida. Older travel guides
and map apps may still mention it.

What Tampa has instead is the City's **Shared Micromobility Program**: dockless e-bikes and
e-scooters run by two companies, **Lime** and **Spin**, under a City contract. There is no
"Tampa bike share app" of the City's own - you use the Lime app or the Spin app.

## How the app e-bikes work

The City of Tampa sets the ground rules for both companies:

- **Unlocking.** Download the Lime or Spin app, create an account, scan the QR code on the bike and
  the trip starts. You end it in the app.
- **Age and ID.** Riders must be 16 or older and have a valid driver's licence or learner's permit.
  The companies can set a higher minimum age in their own terms, so check the app.
- **Speed and range.** The City says the shared vehicles go up to about 15 mph and run 15 to 20 miles
  on a charge.
- **One rider.** No passengers.
- **Helmets** are encouraged but not required, and the apps do not hand you one.
- **Parking.** Trips must end in a designated corral, virtual dock, public bike rack or charging
  station. When the City brought this in in April 2024 it said riders who leave a vehicle outside an
  approved spot face an "out-of-hub" fee from the operator, of up to $5.

Pricing is set by each company and changes, so check the current rate in the app before you unlock.
They are priced for short hops, not for an afternoon out: for anything over an hour or two, compare
the app's quote with a shop's half-day or full-day rate.

## Where you cannot ride an app e-bike in Tampa

This is the part that matters. The City's program page says shared bikes and scooters can be ridden
all over Tampa, **except**:

- **The Tampa Riverwalk** - the 2.6-mile waterfront path from Armature Works to Sparkman Wharf.
- **Bayshore Boulevard** - the City names the whole boulevard.
- **7th Avenue in Ybor City** - Ybor's main nightlife strip.

The City's April 2024 announcement also listed **Hyde Park Village** and **Armature Works** as
no-ride areas. The operators add their own no-ride and no-parking zones in their apps - Spin's, for
example, cover the universities, MacDill Air Force Base and the airports - and the City sets
temporary zones for big events, as it did around both Gasparilla parades in 2026. Check the map in
the app before you plan a route.

In practice that means an app e-bike can get you *to* the Riverwalk or Bayshore, but not along them.
You park in a corral, then walk.

## Shop rentals have no such ban

A bike you rent from a shop is just an e-bike in the eyes of the law, so the shared-program
restrictions do not apply. The City of Tampa's greenways and trails FAQ lists electric bicycles as
allowed on City trails, with a 15 mph limit, and the Riverwalk is part of that trail system. The
slower Riverwalk guideline still applies to everyone - it has been 5 mph, and the path is busy with
people on foot. Our [Tampa routes and rules page](/trails/e-bike-routes-rules-in-tampa-florida/)
lists the mapped paths and the statewide rules, and our
[Florida e-bike laws guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/) covers classes, ages and helmets.

A shop rental also gets you things an app bike will not:

- **A helmet and a lock**, at many shops (ask - it is not universal).
- **A bike that fits**: step-throughs, cruisers, fat tyres, sometimes cargo bikes or kids' bikes.
- **Riders under 16.** The app program is 16-plus; a shop can put a family on bikes, with helmets
  required for anyone under 16 under Florida law.
- **A day or more.** Shops rent by the hour, half day, day or week, and you keep the bike overnight.
- **Delivery.** Several Tampa shops list delivery to hotels and rentals - see
  [Tampa rentals that deliver](/find/delivery/tampa/) and our
  [delivery fee guide](/costs/ebike-delivery-fees-florida/).

The trade-off is that you have to collect and return it during opening hours, the shop will take a
card hold (see [deposits and card holds](/costs/ebike-rental-deposits-and-card-holds/)), and
you cannot simply leave it at the other end of town.

## Which one for which trip

| Your trip | Better option | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hotel to dinner in Ybor or Seminole Heights, one way | App e-bike | Short, one-way, no return trip to a shop. Park in a corral off 7th Avenue |
| Riding the Riverwalk end to end | Shop rental | App bikes are not allowed on it |
| Bayshore Boulevard out and back | Shop rental | App bikes are not allowed on the boulevard |
| Family with children under 16 | Shop rental | The app program is 16-plus |
| Half a day or longer | Shop rental | Built for it, and usually includes a helmet and lock |
| A quick hop between the Channel District and downtown | App e-bike | Unlock, ride, park |
| Getting to a game or concert downtown | App e-bike, if you ride | Check the app for event no-ride zones and full corrals |

## Where to rent from a shop for the waterfront rides

If the Riverwalk and Bayshore are the point of the day, pick up close to them. These are shops in our
directory near each ride; check each listing for what it rents before you go, because not all of them
rent electric bikes.

**For the Riverwalk (downtown).**
[Tampa Riverwalk Rentals](/cities/tampa/tampa-riverwalk-rentals/) launches from the Marriott Water
Street, right on the Riverwalk; its bikes are pedal Coastal Cruisers, listed from $20 on its website
in October 2026, not e-bikes. [City Bike Tampa](/cities/tampa/city-bike-tampa/) on East Cass Street
is a few blocks from the river and lists rentals and delivery - ask whether an e-bike is available.

**For Bayshore Boulevard (South Tampa).**
[Tampa Bay eBikes](/cities/tampa/tampa-bay-ebikes/) has a showroom on South MacDill Avenue, rents

also on South MacDill, and [Outspokin Bicycles](/cities/tampa/outspokin-bicycles-tampa/) on South

**Delivered to you.** [Pedego Electric Bikes Tampa](/cities/tampa/pedego-electric-bikes-tampa/) in

## Riding an app bike well in Tampa

- **Cross the streetcar tracks square-on.** The TECO Line Streetcar runs through downtown, the Channel
  District and Ybor on rails set into the road, and they can catch a narrow tyre. Cross at a right
  angle.
- **Check the corral before you set off.** You cannot end a trip just anywhere, and corrals near busy
  spots fill up on event nights.
- **Bring your own helmet** if you care about having one.
- **Ride in the street, not on busy sidewalks.** The shared bikes must obey traffic laws and yield to
  pedestrians, with an audible signal before passing.
- **Do not drink and ride.** The City's program rules forbid riding under the influence.

## FAQs

### Does Tampa have a bike share?

Not a docked one. Coast Bike Share has closed all its Tampa sites. Tampa's shared bikes are now
dockless e-bikes from Lime and Spin, under the City's Shared Micromobility Program, rented through
each company's app.

### Can you ride a Lime or Spin e-bike on the Tampa Riverwalk?

No. The City of Tampa says shared micromobility vehicles may not be ridden on the Tampa Riverwalk,
Bayshore Boulevard or 7th Avenue in Ybor City. You can ride one to the Riverwalk and park it in a
corral nearby. To ride the Riverwalk itself, rent from a shop or bring your own bike.

### Can I ride a rented e-bike from a shop on the Riverwalk and Bayshore?

Yes. The City allows electric bicycles on its trails, with a 15 mph limit, and the restriction on
the Riverwalk and Bayshore applies to the shared Lime and Spin fleets, not to bikes you rent from a
shop. Ride slowly on the Riverwalk - its guideline has been 5 mph - and use the on-road bike lanes on
Bayshore if you want to go faster.

### How old do you have to be to rent a Lime or Spin bike in Tampa?

At least 16, with a valid driver's licence or learner's permit, under the City's rules. The
companies can set a higher age in their own terms. For younger riders, rent from a shop.

### Where do you park a shared e-bike in Tampa?

In a designated corral, virtual dock, public bike rack or charging station; the app shows where.
Leaving it elsewhere can mean an out-of-hub fee from the operator.

### Is an app bike or a shop rental cheaper in Tampa?

For a short one-way hop, usually the app. For anything over an hour or two, compare: app bikes are
priced by the minute or by ride passes, and shops rent by the hour, half day or day, often with a
helmet and lock. Our [Florida e-bike rental cost guide](/blog/ebike-rental-cost-florida/) shows what
shops typically charge.

## The short version

Use Lime or Spin to get around Tampa - to Ybor, to a game, between the Channel District and downtown -
and park in a corral. Rent from a shop when the ride itself is the plan: the Riverwalk and Bayshore
Boulevard are off limits to app bikes, and a shop bike comes with the helmet, lock, fit and time an
afternoon out needs. For where to ride once you have a bike, see our
[Tampa routes and rules](/trails/e-bike-routes-rules-in-tampa-florida/); for other towns across the
bay, our [Tampa Bay & St. Pete rankings](/blog/best-ebike-rentals-in-tampa-bay-st-pete-florida/).
