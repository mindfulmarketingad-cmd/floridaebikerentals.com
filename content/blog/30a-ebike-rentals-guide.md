If Florida has a spiritual home for e-bike rentals, it is 30A. The 24-mile stretch of coastal
highway between Dune Allen and Inlet Beach has a paved path running its full length, a string of
walkable beach towns spaced almost exactly one snack apart, and a car parking situation bad enough
that riding is genuinely the faster way to get around.

That combination is why Santa Rosa Beach has one of the densest concentrations of e-bike rental
shops anywhere in the state.

## The Timpoochee Trail, briefly

The Timpoochee Trail is a paved multi-use path that parallels County Road 30A for its entire length,
roughly 19 miles of continuous path plus connectors. It is flat, wide enough for two-abreast riding
in most sections, and never far from a beach access, a coffee shop or a public restroom.

It is also genuinely busy. In peak weeks you are sharing it with families on cruisers, runners,
dog walkers, golf carts crossing at intersections, and a great many people who have never ridden an
e-bike before. Ride accordingly: pass on the left with a call or a bell, keep speeds sensible near
town centres, and expect children to change direction without warning.

## The towns, west to east

Riding the whole trail one way takes a comfortable two to three hours with stops. Most visitors do
sections instead.

- **Dune Allen** — the quiet western end. Good for a first ride with kids.
- **Gulf Place / Santa Rosa Beach** — the biggest concentration of rental shops, restaurants and
  parking. A sensible base.
- **Blue Mountain Beach** — a short climb by 30A standards, which is to say barely a hill, plus one
  of the best ice cream stops on the route.
- **Grayton Beach** — old Florida, sandy streets, and the entrance to Grayton Beach State Park.
- **WaterColor and Seaside** — the postcard section. Seaside's central square is the single busiest
  point on the trail; dismount and walk it in season.
- **Seagrove Beach** — quieter, good beach accesses.
- **Seacrest and Alys Beach** — the architecture stretch, white buildings and very tidy verges.
- **Rosemary Beach** — cobbled streets, the eastern anchor town.
- **Inlet Beach** — the eastern end, with access toward Camp Helen State Park.

A classic half-day: rent in Santa Rosa Beach, ride east to Seaside for lunch, continue to Rosemary
Beach for coffee, then ride back. That is roughly 20 miles round trip, which is a long day on a
regular bike and an easy afternoon on an e-bike. This is exactly the ride that converts people.

## Why e-bikes dominate here

Three reasons, in order of importance.

**Distance between towns.** Grayton to Rosemary is about nine miles. On a beach cruiser with a
seven-year-old, that is a negotiation. On e-bikes, it is a pleasant hour.

**Wind.** The coastal wind on 30A is real and usually favours you in exactly one direction. Pedal
assist removes the misery of the return leg.

**Parking.** Public beach parking on 30A fills early and stays full. Riding from your rental house
to a beach access is frequently faster than driving to one, and always less stressful.

## What to rent

**Class 2 cruisers** are the default and the right choice for most visitors: upright riding
position, throttle for the hills that are not really hills, fat enough tyres for the sandy path
edges.

**Step-through frames** if anyone in the group is nervous about mounting, or riding in beach clothes.

**Cargo or long-tail e-bikes** if you have two kids under about seven. One adult can carry both,
which is far less stressful than shepherding two small riders along a busy trail.

**Kids' e-bikes** are widely available but check the shop's minimum age. Many require 16 for
independent riding regardless of bike size.

Ask about **battery range**. Most rentals will do 25 - 40 miles of mixed riding, which is plenty for
a full day of 30A. If you plan the whole trail out and back plus town riding, ask whether the shop
swaps batteries.

## Booking in peak season

30A's peak is summer, plus spring break and holiday weeks. In those windows:

- Book **at least a week ahead**, more for four or more bikes.
- Ask about **delivery to your rental house**. Nearly every 30A shop delivers, many include it, and
  it removes the entire problem of transporting bikes.
- Confirm the **pickup and return window**. Delivery rentals are usually dropped the morning of day
  one and collected the morning after your last day.
- Check whether **helmets, locks and lights** are included. Riding back from dinner in Seaside after
  dark without lights is both illegal and genuinely dangerous.

Off-season — late autumn and January — you can often walk up and rent same day, and rates soften.

## Practical trail notes

- **Cross 30A at marked crossings.** Traffic moves faster than the surroundings suggest and sight
  lines around the dune vegetation are poor.
- **Lock the bikes, always.** E-bikes are targets. Use the shop's lock and secure through the frame,
  not just the wheel.
- **Watch for sand on the path** after wind, especially near the western dune crossings. It behaves
  like gravel.
- **Bring more water than you think.** There is shade, but not much, and Gulf humidity is punishing
  in July.
- **Golf carts share the road**, not the path, and cross the path constantly at driveways.
