Every winter St. Augustine covers its historic district in roughly three million white lights.
Nights of Lights is the city's busiest season, and the hardest thing about it is not the crowds but
the parking. A bike solves most of that. An e-bike solves it from farther away, so you can stay on
Anastasia Island or at St. Augustine Beach and still ride in for the evening.

This guide covers the 2026 dates, how a bike fits into the night, the downtown spots where you
can't ride, Florida's lighting rules after dark and the rental shops we track in the area.

## Nights of Lights 2026: the dates

- **Season:** Saturday, November 21, 2026 to Monday, January 18, 2027. The lights come on at dusk every day and stay lit past midnight.
- **Light-Up! Night:** Saturday, November 21. Entertainment starts at 4:00 p.m. by City Hall and the Lightner Museum (75 King St.), moves to the Plaza de la Constitución at 5:30 p.m., and the switch is flipped at 6:30 p.m. Admission is free.
- **Christmas Parade:** Saturday, December 5, 2026, at 9:00 a.m.
- **Regatta of Lights:** Saturday, December 12, 2026, at 6:00 p.m., on the bayfront.

Dates and times are from the City of St. Augustine and Visit St. Augustine. Check
[citystaug.com](https://www.citystaug.com/515/Light-Up-Night) before you go, since event details can
change.

## Bike in, then walk the lights

The city describes Nights of Lights as a walking event, and it is right. The lit streets are
narrow, packed with people after dark, and the best parts, St. George Street and the inside of the
Plaza, are closed to bikes anyway (see the next section). The way to use a bike is to **ride to
the edge of the historic district, lock up, and walk**.

That still saves a lot. The city has more than 30 bike-parking locations near popular downtown
spots, and a bike skips both the downtown parking hunt and the shuttle queue. On busy nights the
city runs free park-and-ride shuttles from lots outside downtown; on Light-Up! Night the
Anastasia Island shuttle drops people at the east end of the Bridge of Lions and they walk across.
If you are staying on the island, riding to the bridge and on into town takes the same route
without the wait.

An e-bike earns its keep on the longer trips. From the rental shops on A1A Beach Boulevard in St.
Augustine Beach to the Bridge of Lions is a ride of several miles, and doing it at night after a day
out is much easier with pedal assist. If you are only moving around downtown, a regular bike or
your feet will do.

New this season: the city's bikeshare, run by local company DRIFTERS, launches on Friday,
November 6, 2026, with 80 pedal bikes and 10 pedal-assist bikes docked across downtown, West King
Street, Anastasia Boulevard and St. Augustine Beach. Rentals are through its app, by the hour or on
a membership. With only ten e-bikes in the fleet, book a shop rental if you specifically want
pedal assist on a busy night.

## Where you can't ride in downtown St. Augustine

[City Code section 24-106](https://library.municode.com/fl/st._augustine/codes/code_of_ordinances)
treats e-bikes as bicycles. You can ride them on the roads, on designated shared use paths, and on
sidewalks that are at least 8 feet wide, with these exceptions, which are pedestrian-only:

- **St. George Street** and the walkways on or beside it, between Orange Street (the City Gates) and Cathedral Place. This is the heart of the lights, so walk it.
- **The inside walkways of the Plaza de la Constitución.** The sidewalk around the edge of the Plaza is not included.
- **The seawall along Avenida Menendez.**

Two more rules matter for visitors:

- **No group sidewalk rides with a guide.** Three or more bikes riding together with a tour guide must use the traffic lanes, not the sidewalk.
- **Bikes left unattended on public property can be impounded.** Lock up at a rack or designated parking area, not to a lamp post or railing.

## Riding after dark: the lighting rule

Nights of Lights means riding home in the dark, and
[section 316.2065](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.2065) is specific. Between sunset
and sunrise a bike, electric or not, needs:

- a **white front light** visible from at least 500 feet, and
- a **red rear reflector and a red rear light** visible from 600 feet.

Many rental e-bikes have lights built in, but not all of them, so ask when you book and check
that both work before you set off. A bright jacket helps, because drivers downtown are looking at
the lights, not for cyclists. Our [Florida e-bike laws guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/) covers the
rest of the state rules.

## A simple evening plan

1. **Pick up late afternoon.** Collect the bike before the shop closes and check the lights and lock.
2. **Ride in before dusk.** The lights come on at dusk, and roads into downtown get busier as it gets dark. Arriving in daylight makes it easier to find a rack.
3. **Lock up on the edge of the district** and walk St. George Street, the Plaza and the bayfront.
4. **Ride back with lights on**, at an easy pace. Crowds spill into the streets after dark, so be ready to stop or walk the bike.

If you would rather see the lights on a guided outing, our

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you bike through Nights of Lights?

You can ride to it and around the roads at the edge of the historic district, but St. George Street
and the Plaza's inside walkways are pedestrian-only, and the city recommends seeing the lights on
foot. Bike in, lock up and walk.

### Can you ride an e-bike on the sidewalk in St. Augustine?

On sidewalks at least 8 feet wide, yes, except on or beside St. George Street between Orange Street
and Cathedral Place, inside the Plaza de la Constitución and on the Avenida Menendez seawall. That
is section 24-106. Pedestrians come first, and you have to give an audible signal before passing
someone on a sidewalk
([section 316.2065](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.2065)).

### Is there bike parking for Nights of Lights?

The city maintains more than 30 bike-parking locations near popular downtown destinations. Use a
rack or a designated parking area. Section 24-106 lets the city impound a bike left unattended on
public property.

### Do I need lights on a bike at night in Florida?

Yes. Between sunset and sunrise [section 316.2065](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.2065)
requires a white front light visible from 500 feet, plus a red rear reflector and a red rear light
visible from 600 feet.

### Does St. Augustine have a bike share?

Yes. The DRIFTERS city bikeshare launches November 6, 2026, with 80 pedal bikes and 10 pedal-assist
bikes across downtown, West King Street, Anastasia Boulevard and St. Augustine Beach, rented through
an app.
