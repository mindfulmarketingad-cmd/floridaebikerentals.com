Every Florida rental contract names a class. Most renters sign without knowing what it means, then
discover on the trail that their bike behaves differently from their friend's. The three-class
system is genuinely simple, and knowing it helps you rent the right bike and stay legal on shared
paths.

## The three classes

**Class 1 — pedal assist only, up to 20 mph.** The motor helps only while you are pedalling, and
stops helping at 20 mph. No throttle. This is the class with the widest trail access anywhere in the
United States, because it behaves most like a bicycle.

**Class 2 — pedal assist plus throttle, up to 20 mph.** Same 20 mph ceiling, but you can also move
the bike without pedalling using a thumb throttle or twist grip. This is what most Florida holiday
rentals are.

**Class 3 — pedal assist only, up to 28 mph.** Faster assist, no throttle, and a speedometer is
required. Fastest to cover ground, most restricted on shared paths, and subject to a minimum
operating age of 16 in Florida.

All three top out at a **750 watt motor** and must have functioning pedals. Beyond those limits, it
is not an electric bicycle in the legal sense, and different rules apply entirely.

## Pedal assist versus throttle, in practice

**Pedal assist** reads your pedalling and adds power proportionally. It feels like a strong tailwind
that appears whenever you push. Most bikes have three to five assist levels; higher levels drain
battery faster.

**A throttle** is a motorcycle-style control: press it, the bike moves, pedals optional. Useful for
starting from a stop on a hill, moving a heavy loaded cargo bike out of a driveway, or letting a
rider with a knee injury keep up.

The practical difference for a holiday rental is what happens when someone gets tired. On a Class 1,
a tired rider still has to pedal. On a Class 2, they can coast on the throttle for the last two
miles. For families and mixed-ability groups, that flexibility is the whole argument for Class 2.

The counter-argument: throttles teach bad habits fast. Riders who use the throttle from a standstill
in a crowd, or who twist it while looking down at the controls, cause most of the low-speed
collisions rental shops see.

## Which class should you rent in Florida?

**Beach town cruising with family — Class 2.** Throttle flexibility, 20 mph is plenty, and it is
permitted almost everywhere a bike is.

**Long paved rail trails, distance riding — Class 1 or 3.** Class 1 has the broadest access. Class 3
covers more ground, but check the trail's rules first: many multi-use paths in Florida allow Class 1
and 2 while excluding Class 3.

**Riding with children who are riding their own bikes — Class 1.** Nobody in the group needs 28 mph,
and the absence of a throttle removes a temptation.

**Anyone under 16 — Class 1 or 2 only.** Class 3 has a 16 year old minimum operating age in Florida,
and shop policy is often stricter still.

## Battery range and what the class does not tell you

Class says nothing about range. That comes from battery capacity, usually printed as watt-hours
(Wh), plus your weight, the assist level and the wind.

Rough guide for a typical Florida rental with a 500 - 700 Wh battery:

- **Low assist, flat paved path:** 40 - 60 miles.
- **Mixed assist, some throttle, a bit of wind:** 25 - 40 miles.
- **High assist and heavy throttle use:** 15 - 25 miles.
- **Loaded cargo bike with two children:** cut all of the above by a third.

For nearly every Florida day ride that is more than enough. If you plan to ride the full Timpoochee
Trail out and back with town riding on top, ask the shop about range explicitly, and whether they
carry a spare battery.

## Questions worth asking at the counter

- **What class is this bike?**
- **Where is that class not allowed locally** — trails, sidewalks, state parks?
- **How many assist levels, and which is the sensible default?**
- **What is the realistic range** for the riding I described?
- **How do I cut power in a hurry?** (Both brakes; on most bikes, either lever kills the motor.)

## One thing to try before you leave the shop

Ride a slow circle in the car park with the assist at its lowest setting, then at its highest.
The difference between assist levels on an e-bike is much larger than most first-timers expect, and
discovering that at low speed on gravel is far better than discovering it while pulling into traffic.

Then check the brakes properly. Rental e-bikes are heavy — often 50 to 70 lb — and stopping distance
is not what you remember from a beach cruiser.
