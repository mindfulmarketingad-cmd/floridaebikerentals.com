Sanibel is one of the best places in Florida to ride a bike. It has more than 26 miles of paved
shared use path, kept apart from the road for almost the whole way. The island is flat, half of it
is wildlife refuge, and the League of American Bicyclists rates it a Gold-level Bicycle Friendly
Community. It also has one of the strictest e-bike rules in the state, and that rule decides which
bike you should rent.

Here is what the rule says and who it applies to, followed by the rides worth doing and what to ask
a rental shop before you book.

## The rule: Class 1 e-bikes only on the shared use path

The City of Sanibel allows **only Class 1 e-bikes** on its shared use path. The city's own
[shared-use path page](https://www.mysanibel.com/318/Bike-Hike-on-the-Shared-Use-Path) says a Class 1
bike helps only while you pedal, has **no throttle**, and stops helping at **20 mph**. Class 2 bikes
(which have a throttle) and Class 3 bikes (which help up to 28 mph) are not allowed on the path. The
code says the same thing.
[Ordinance 24-014](https://mcclibraryfunctions.azurewebsites.us/api/ordinanceDownload/10937/1324703/pdf),
adopted September 9, 2024 and effective October 1, 2024, amended section 66-165 so that the ban on
motor vehicles on the path does not apply to a person age 16 or older on a Class 1 electric-assisted
bicycle. Our [e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/) explains the three classes.

The rest of the rule for the path:

- **Riders must be 16 or older.** That is section 66-165. It covers personal bikes as well as rentals.
- **Nothing else with a motor.** Motorized scooters, mopeds and other micromobility devices are banned from the path. The exception is a mobility device used by a person with a disability.
- **Pedestrians have the right of way.** Section 66-164 says yield, and give an audible signal before you pass.
- **Headphones.** [Section 316.304](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.304) prohibits operating a vehicle while wearing a headset or headphones, other than a hearing aid. A one-ear headset used for a phone call is an exception. The city's [bike brochure](https://www.mysanibel.com/DocumentCenter/View/144/Bike-or-Hike-Brochure-PDF) also tells riders to keep one ear free.
- **No e-bikes on beaches or dunes.** [Ordinance 22-006](https://mcclibraryfunctions.azurewebsites.us/api/ordinanceDownload/10937/1170971/pdf) amended section 74-182 so that an engine-powered or battery-powered vehicle, including an electric-assisted bicycle, may not be operated on a beach or dune. A mobility device actually being used by a person with a disability is the exception.
- **Posted speed limits.** Section 66-164 lets the city manager set path speed limits, and none of them may be higher than 20 mph. Obey the signs. Riding carelessly on the path is its own violation of that section.

In a May 2026 safety note reported by the
[Island Reporter](https://www.captivasanibel.com/news/2026/05/safe-cycling-on-sanibel-what-to-know-on-sup/),
Sanibel police also asked cyclists to ride single file and to keep groups to three or four. That is
police guidance. We did not find it written as a number in the code. Many sections of the path are
only 6 to 8 feet wide and cross driveways and side streets with no traffic lights.

Florida law is the baseline everywhere else. An e-bike with a motor of less than 750 watts and
working pedals counts as a bicycle under
[sections 316.003](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.003) and
[316.20655](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.20655), so you don't need a licence or
registration, and every rider or passenger under 16 must wear a helmet under
[section 316.2065](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.2065). Our
[Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/) covers the statewide rules. Sanibel's path rule
is stricter than state law, and it is enforced.

## What the rule means when you rent

**Ask what class the bike is.** Many popular rental models leave the factory with a throttle, which
makes them Class 2. Sanibel shops get around this by setting their fleets up as Class 1. Finnimore's,
for example, says it removes the throttle from every Aventon Pace 500 it rents and leaves the
pedal assist capped at 20 mph. If you rent off the island, or bring your own bike over the
causeway, check for a throttle before you ride the path.

**Check the shop's minimum age, not just the city's.** The city sets 16 as the minimum age. Some
shops set it higher: Billy's Rentals, for one, requires e-bike renters to be 18. Children under 16
can't ride an e-bike on the path at all, so a family with younger children will need pedal bikes,
trailers or tag-alongs for them. Finnimore's, for one, rents all three.

**Expect a test ride.** Billy's asks e-bike renters to come in for a fitting and test ride before it
delivers, and to show they can handle the bike. That is worth doing anyway. An e-bike handles
differently from a pedal bike, and the police suggest practising somewhere open before you join a
busy path.

## The best rides on Sanibel

The path network follows the island's main roads: Periwinkle Way along the bay side, Sanibel-Captiva
Road ("San-Cap") to the west, and the north-south links between them, Causeway Boulevard, Tarpon
Bay Road and Rabbit Road. The distances below are rough figures from the middle of Periwinkle Way,
where several rental shops are, and come from shop route maps.

**Lighthouse Beach Park, about 4 miles.** The east-end ride. It follows Periwinkle Way past shops
and restaurants to the Sanibel Lighthouse and the fishing pier at the tip of the island. It is the
busiest stretch of path, so go mid-morning or late afternoon on weekdays for the calmest riding.

**J.N. "Ding" Darling National Wildlife Refuge, about 3 miles.** The refuge's Wildlife Drive is a
four-mile, one-way paved road shared by cars, walkers and bikes. The U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service
lists a 15 mph speed limit, closure every Friday, and a rule that visitors must be off refuge land
within 30 minutes of sunset
([visit Ding Darling](https://www.fws.gov/refuge/jn-ding-darling/visit-us)). It starts at the
visitor centre off San-Cap and comes out on Wulfert Road. Riders get back on the path and return
east along San-Cap, a loop of roughly 8 miles from the visitor centre. The refuge's biking page says
e-bikes are allowed on refuge roads and trails that are open to traditional bicycles, where that
use fits the refuge's purpose and the refuge manager finds it compatible
([biking at Ding Darling](https://www.fws.gov/refuge/jn-ding-darling/visit-us/activities/biking)).
Follow the signs at the fee booth. The Service's visit page lists Wildlife Drive at $10 per vehicle
and does not print a separate bicycle price, so confirm the cyclist fee at the booth rather than
relying on an older figure.

- **Wildlife Drive is closed every Friday**, all year, holidays included.
- Gate hours change with the season, so check the refuge's page before you go.
- Some refuge trails ask you to walk bikes on boardwalks, and a few are closed to bikes altogether. The smaller **Bailey Tract** off Tarpon Bay Road is free and open to bikes, sunrise to sunset, on the same visit page.

Early morning is the best time for wildlife, and the low tide brings out the wading birds.

**Bowman's Beach and Blind Pass, about 7 miles.** San-Cap is the island's quiet side: fewer shops,
less shade, more mangrove. Mile markers along the road count up to Blind Pass at mile 7, with the
Bowman's Beach turn just before mile 5. The path ends about a tenth of a mile before the Blind Pass
bridge. Lock the bike at a rack, because e-bikes are not allowed on the sand.

**The full island, about 25 to 30 miles.** Riding the whole path network out and back adds up to
about 30 miles. That is a long day on a pedal bike but well within a modern e-bike battery on an
island with no hills. Carry water: the San-Cap stretch has little shade.

## Riding on to Captiva

Captiva has **no shared use path**. Past Blind Pass you ride on Captiva Drive, which has a bike lane
but is narrow and winding, and in places plants grow into the lane. It is about 3.5 to 5 miles from
Turner Beach at Blind Pass to the beach at the north end, and about 11 or 12 miles from mid-Periwinkle
to Captiva village. Confident adults handle it fine. With children, or in heavy traffic, stop at
Turner Beach and turn back.

Sanibel's Class 1 rule is written for its path. On Captiva's roads, Florida law treats e-bikes as
bicycles. Since you will ride Sanibel's path to get there, the Class 1 limit applies to the whole
trip anyway.

## Renting on Sanibel

Our [Sanibel bike rentals page](/cities/sanibel/) lists every shop we track on the island, ranked
by Google rating and review count, with hours, delivery and directions. For more choice, there are
more shops in [Fort Myers Beach](/cities/fort-myers-beach/) and [Fort Myers](/cities/fort-myers/)
across the bay. What the island shops publish (checked November 1, 2026):

- **Delivery to Sanibel and Captiva is common.** Finnimore's charges $10 round trip on Sanibel and $25 on Captiva, with a two-day minimum. Island Breeze eBikes delivers to both islands or offers free pickup at its Rabbit Road shop. Billy's delivers from Periwinkle Way after your test ride.
- **Published e-bike rates** start at $50 for four hours and $80 a day at Finnimore's, and $75 for four hours and $90 for eight hours at Billy's. Weekly rates at both are $250 to $300. Prices change, so confirm when you book. For the wider picture, see our [Florida rental cost guide](/blog/ebike-rental-cost-florida/) and [delivery fee guide](/costs/ebike-delivery-fees-florida/).
- **Book ahead in season.** Winter and spring are peak season. Finnimore's says its e-bikes book up faster than anything else it rents, and Billy's suggests checking availability before you travel.

When you book, ask:

1. **Is the bike Class 1 as you rent it?** On Sanibel the answer should be yes.
2. **What is your minimum age for e-bikes?** It may be 18, not 16.
3. **Do you deliver where I'm staying, and what's the minimum rental?**
4. **Is a helmet, lock and path map included?** Several island shops include all three.

## When to ride

November to April is the most comfortable time to ride, with lower humidity and fewer afternoon
storms. It is also when the paths are busiest. From June to September, ride early and carry plenty
of water. Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30. Parking near the beaches is
paid by the hour and hard to find on busy days, which is one more reason visitors ride instead.

## FAQs

### Are e-bikes allowed on Sanibel's bike path?

Only Class 1 e-bikes: pedal-assist bikes with no throttle and a top assisted speed of 20 mph, ridden
by someone 16 or older. That is the city's
[shared-use path page](https://www.mysanibel.com/318/Bike-Hike-on-the-Shared-Use-Path) and section
66-165, as amended by Ordinance 24-014. Class 2 (throttle) and Class 3 (28 mph) e-bikes are not
allowed on the path.

### Can a 14-year-old ride an e-bike on Sanibel?

Not on the shared use path. Section 66-165 sets 16 as the minimum age for an e-bike rider on the
path, and some rental shops require renters to be 18. Younger children can ride pedal bikes, and
[Florida law](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.2065) requires a helmet for every
bicycle rider or passenger under 16.

### Can I bring a throttle e-bike to Sanibel?

You can bring it, but a bike with a throttle is a Class 2 and can't be ridden on the shared use
path. Some riders physically remove or disconnect the throttle to run the bike as a Class 1. If in
doubt, rent a Class 1 bike on the island.

### Can you ride an e-bike through Ding Darling?

Bicycles are allowed on the four-mile Wildlife Drive. The
[Service's biking page](https://www.fws.gov/refuge/jn-ding-darling/visit-us/activities/biking) allows
e-bikes where traditional bicycles are allowed if the refuge manager finds that use compatible, so
follow the posted signs. Wildlife Drive is closed every Friday. Confirm the cyclist entrance fee at
the booth; the refuge's visit page lists the vehicle fee and not a separate bicycle price.

### Can you ride e-bikes on the beach on Sanibel?

No. Section 74-182, as amended by
[Ordinance 22-006](https://mcclibraryfunctions.azurewebsites.us/api/ordinanceDownload/10937/1170971/pdf),
prohibits an electric-assisted bicycle on a beach or dune. Lock the bike at a beach access rack and
walk the last few yards.

### Is there a bike path on Captiva?

No. Sanibel's path ends just before the Blind Pass bridge. On Captiva you ride in the bike lane on
Captiva Drive, which is narrow and winding, so it suits confident adult riders better than young
children.
