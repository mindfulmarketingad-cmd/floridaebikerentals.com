Ninety percent of bad e-bike rental experiences trace back to something that took thirty seconds to
check at the counter. This is the list. Work through it once and it becomes automatic.

## Before you book

**Confirm they are open.** Seasonal hours in Florida beach towns change without much warning, and
Google hours lag reality. Call. Every listing in our [directory](/partners/) carries the shop's
phone number for exactly this reason.

**Ask what class the bikes are** and whether that class is allowed where you intend to ride. See
[e-bike classes explained](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/).

**Ask the minimum age** for every rider in your group, and what they offer younger children.

**Get the total price, not the headline rate.** Ask specifically about helmets, locks, child seats,
delivery, damage waiver and tax.

**Ask about the card hold** — the authorisation amount per bike, and how long it takes to release.

**Ask what happens if it rains.** Policies range from full refunds to nothing at all.

## The contract terms that actually matter

Read four clauses before you sign. They are the ones that cost people money.

1. **Liability for damage.** What is your maximum exposure? Is there an excess or is it full
   replacement value?
2. **Theft.** Almost always excluded from damage waivers. If the bike is stolen, you are usually
   liable for the full replacement cost, even if it was locked.
3. **Late return.** Per-hour or per-day, and how it is calculated.
4. **Where you may ride.** Some contracts prohibit beaches, off-road use, or leaving a defined area,
   and riding outside it voids everything.

If a shop will not show you the agreement before payment, rent elsewhere.

## The six-point bike check

Do this in the shop, before you ride away. It takes under two minutes.

**1. Brakes.** Squeeze both levers hard. They should firm up well before touching the grip. Roll the
bike forward and brake — no grinding, no pulling to one side. E-bikes are heavy and stopping
distance matters more than you expect.

**2. Tyres.** Press hard with your thumb. A properly inflated e-bike tyre barely gives. Soft tyres
kill range, handle badly in sand and pinch-flat on kerbs.

**3. Battery charge.** Look at the display. Ask what the indicator means and what range you should
expect at the level you will ride. If it is showing three of five bars at pickup, ask for a
different bike.

**4. Motor cutoff.** With the bike stationary and the display on, squeeze a brake lever. On most
e-bikes this cuts motor power. Know how it behaves before you need it.

**5. Lights.** Turn them on. If you might return after dusk — and on a 24-hour rental you will —
they are both a legal requirement and the thing that keeps you visible on a dark beach road.

**6. Quick releases and the seat clamp.** Everything that should be tight should be tight. Wheels,
seat post, handlebar stem.

## Fit

**Saddle height.** Sitting on the saddle with a pedal at its lowest point, your leg should be almost
straight with a slight bend. Too low is the most common rental setup and it makes long rides
painful.

**Reach.** You should be able to hold the grips with a slight elbow bend without stretching.

**Brake lever reach.** Small hands need the levers adjusted inward. Most shops can do this in
seconds if you ask, and almost nobody asks.

**Kickstand and step-over.** If anyone in your group has mobility limits, ask for a step-through
frame explicitly.

## Photograph the bike

Before you leave, take four photos on your phone: both sides, the display showing the charge, and
any existing damage — scratched frame, scuffed rims, torn saddle. Get the shop staff to acknowledge
existing damage on the paperwork.

This takes 30 seconds and settles every dispute that can happen at return time.

## Pack list for a Florida ride

- **Water**, more than you think. One bottle per person per hour in summer.
- **Sunscreen** for shoulders, neck and the backs of your legs.
- **A phone mount or secure pocket** — not loose in a rear pocket on a bumpy path.
- **The shop's phone number saved**, not just the address.
- **A lock, and a plan for where you will park.**
- **Cash or card for the stop you have not planned yet.**

## While you ride

- Start at the **lowest assist level** for the first mile. Get used to the power delivery before you
  turn it up.
- **Brake earlier** than instinct says. Weight plus speed equals distance.
- **Watch the range** on the display rather than the battery bars alone, if the bike shows it.
- **Do not ride an e-bike into salt water** or deep sand. Both are excluded from most agreements and
  both destroy motors.
- **Lock it every single time**, even for a five-minute stop.

## At return

- Return with the bike on the same charge level the contract asks for, if it specifies one.
- Get **written confirmation** that the bike came back undamaged before you leave.
- Confirm the **card hold release** — ask when it will drop off, and note the date.

## The short version

Call ahead. Read the damage and theft clauses. Check brakes, tyres, battery, lights, cutoff and fit.
Photograph the bike. Take water. Lock it every time.
