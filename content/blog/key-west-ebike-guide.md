Key West is four miles long, two miles wide, and almost entirely flat. It also has some of the worst
parking in Florida and a street grid full of one-way turns that make driving slower than riding. The
island has more bike rental shops per square mile than anywhere else in the state, and there is a
reason.

## Why ride instead of drive

**Parking.** Old Town residential parking is permit-only in large sections, garages fill by
mid-morning in season, and the meters are enforced seriously. A bike goes where the car cannot.

**Distance.** Everything a visitor wants to see sits within a 15-minute ride: Mallory Square, Duval
Street, the Southernmost Point, Hemingway's house, Fort Zachary Taylor, Smathers Beach, the
Historic Seaport. Nothing is far enough apart to need a car and nothing is close enough together to
walk it all in Florida heat.

**Heat and humidity.** This is the case for an *electric* bike rather than a pedal one. Key West in
August at midday is brutal on foot and merely pleasant with assist and a breeze.

## The classic loop

A three to four hour ride that covers the island:

1. Start in Old Town near the Historic Seaport.
2. Ride south-west through the residential streets to the **Southernmost Point** (early, unless you
   enjoy queuing for the photo).
3. Continue to **Fort Zachary Taylor** — the best swimming beach on the island and worth the park
   entry.
4. Loop back along the waterfront to **Mallory Square**.
5. Head east on the **Atlantic side** past Higgs Beach and White Street Pier to **Smathers Beach**.
6. Return through the quieter streets of the Meadows or along the **Key West First State Bike Route**
   toward the seaport.

That is roughly 12 to 14 miles with stops. Add the ride out toward Stock Island if you want
distance.

## Where you can and cannot ride

Key West regulates bikes more actively than most Florida towns, and rules change. The stable
generalisations:

- **Duval Street** is chaotic. Legal to ride in sections, unpleasant in most of them, and walking
  the bike through the busiest blocks is the sane choice in the evening.
- **Sidewalks in the commercial core** are generally off limits to riding.
- **Beaches** are not for riding on. Lock at the racks.
- **One-way streets** apply to bikes. Salsa-ing the wrong way up a one-way is the fastest way to
  meet a Key West police officer.
- **Bike lanes and the North and South Roosevelt paths** are the comfortable ways across the island.

Ask your rental shop for the current local rules — they get told first when something changes.

## Timing your ride around the cruise ships

Cruise ships dock in the morning and Old Town's foot traffic roughly doubles for a few hours around
Mallory Square and lower Duval. If you want photographs without crowds, ride before 9am. If you want
the atmosphere, ride mid-afternoon. Either way, do not plan to move quickly through Old Town between
about 10am and 2pm on a two-ship day.

Sunset at Mallory Square is worth doing once, and is the single worst time to try to ride through
that area.

## Locking up: take this seriously

Bicycle theft is a genuine, persistent problem in Key West, and e-bikes are the most attractive
targets on the island. Every rental agreement you sign will make you liable for a stolen bike, and
the replacement value is well north of $1,500.

- Use the lock the shop gives you, through the **frame**, to a fixed rack.
- Never leave a bike overnight on the street. Bring it into your accommodation or use a locked area.
- Take the **battery** with you if it detaches easily and you are stopping for more than a few
  minutes.
- Photograph the bike and lock when you park it.

## What to rent

**A Class 2 cruiser** covers 95 percent of Key West visits — upright, comfortable, throttle for
pulling away from junctions in traffic.

**Fat tyres** are unnecessary here. The riding is paved.

**Baskets matter more than you would think**, because you will buy things. Ask for a bike with a
front basket or a rack.

**Rent for 24 hours, not 4.** The evening ride back from dinner is the best part of the day, and the
price difference is usually small. Confirm the bike has working lights — required after dark, and
Key West's side streets are dim.

## Practical notes

- **Rain.** Afternoon storms in summer are brief and heavy. Most shops are relaxed about riders
  sheltering; the bikes handle a soaking, the riders less so.
- **Chickens.** They are everywhere, they have right of way in practice, and they move unpredictably.
- **Scooters and Conch trains** share the same narrow streets. Assume you are not seen.
- **Bring a lock-friendly bag** rather than a rucksack in the heat.
