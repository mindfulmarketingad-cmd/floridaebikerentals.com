Delivery is the quiet upgrade in Florida e-bike rentals. It removes the single most annoying part of
renting bikes with a family - moving four of them in a hire car - and in beach towns it is close to
standard.

## What it costs

- **Included:** many beach town shops deliver free inside a set radius, commonly 3 to 10 miles, and
  a good number include it outright on rentals of three days or more.
- **Flat fee:** $20 to $50 each way is the usual charge outside the free radius.
- **Per bike:** less common, but some shops charge $10 to $15 per bike for delivery and collection.

The pattern to look for is a free radius plus a fee beyond it. Ask two questions: *what is the free
radius, and what is the fee for my address?*

## What delivery actually includes

A good delivery is not just a drop-off. Expect:

- Bikes brought to the property, sized and adjusted for each rider.
- Helmets, locks and lights supplied and fitted.
- A short handover: how the assist levels work, how to charge, what to do if something breaks.
- Collection at an agreed time on your last morning.

If a shop will not size the bikes at delivery, that is worth knowing before you book - a badly set
saddle makes a week of riding uncomfortable.

## When it is worth paying for

**Renting three or more bikes.** The maths is simple: you are not fitting three e-bikes and a child
seat into a mid-size rental car, and a bike rack for a week costs more than the delivery fee.

**Staying more than a couple of days.** Delivery on a week-long rental typically works out at a few
dollars a day.

**Staying somewhere with parking constraints.** Anywhere on 30A, Key West, or a barrier island where
you would otherwise be driving to a shop and back twice.

## When to collect from the shop instead

**Short rentals.** For a two-hour or half-day ride, you are usually going to the shop anyway.

**When you want to try before you commit.** Trying two frame styles in the car park is easier at the
shop than on your driveway.

**When the fee is per bike and you are renting several.** Occasionally the maths flips.

## Practical notes

Confirm the delivery **window**, not just the day - "Saturday morning" is the usual level of
precision, and a tighter window is worth asking for if you have plans.

Confirm **where the bikes will be left** if nobody is home, and whether they will be locked. On a
week-long rental, ask where you are expected to store them overnight; a condo garage is fine, a
beachfront balcony is a theft claim waiting to happen.

Confirm the **collection time on your last day**. Most shops collect in the morning, which means the
final day is not really a riding day.

## FAQs

### Do Florida e-bike rental shops deliver?

Many do, especially in beach towns where visitors stay in rental houses and condos. Delivery is
often free within a few miles of the shop and charged beyond that. Browse the shops whose profiles
list delivery on our [delivery page](/find/ebike-rentals-with-delivery-in-florida/).

### How much does e-bike delivery cost in Florida?

Typically free inside a set radius of 3 to 10 miles, and $20 to $50 each way beyond it. Some shops
include delivery outright on rentals of three days or more, and a few charge per bike instead.

### Is e-bike delivery worth paying for?

For three or more bikes, or any rental longer than a couple of days, almost always. Moving several
e-bikes in a hire car is impractical, and a rack for the week usually costs more than the fee.

### Will they set the bikes up when they deliver?

A good shop sizes each bike to its rider, fits helmets, supplies locks and lights, and walks you
through the controls. Ask before booking - a shop that only drops bikes at the kerb is a different
product.

### When do they collect the bikes?

Usually the morning of your last day, which means that day is not a riding day. If you want a final
morning ride, agree the collection time when you book.
