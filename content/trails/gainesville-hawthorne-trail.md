The Gainesville-Hawthorne State Trail runs sixteen miles from Boulware Springs Park on the edge of
Gainesville south-east to the town of Hawthorne, cutting straight across the rim of Paynes Prairie
Preserve State Park.

It is the best wildlife ride in Florida on pavement. Not the prettiest coastline, not the longest
corridor - but the trail where you are most likely to look up from the handlebars at a wild horse, a
herd of bison, or an alligator crossing the path in front of you.

## The route

From Boulware Springs the trail climbs briefly out of the park, then runs along the northern and
eastern rim of Paynes Prairie, past the La Chua and Prairie Creek areas, through Lochloosa hammock
country, and on to the Hawthorne trailhead at the south-eastern end.

The prairie overlooks in the first six or seven miles are the highlight. There are observation
platforms and boardwalk spurs off the main trail, and they are worth every stop.

## Paynes Prairie and its wildlife

Paynes Prairie is a 21,000-acre wet prairie basin managed as a state preserve, and it carries free
ranging herds:

- **Bison and wild horses**, descended from herds reintroduced to the preserve.
- **Alligators**, in large numbers, particularly near the La Chua end.
- **Sandhill cranes**, herons, and in winter migratory birds in serious numbers.
- **Snakes**, which use the warm asphalt.

Give animals room. Do not attempt to ride past a bison or a horse on the trail - stop, wait, and let
it move. Alligators on or beside the path are routine; treat them the same way.

## The riding

Unusually for Florida, this trail has gradient. The section out of Boulware Springs and the rolling
hammock country toward Hawthorne include short, genuine climbs - modest anywhere else, noticeable
here, and steeper than anything on the rail trails to the south.

The surface is paved throughout and generally good, with some root heave in the wooded sections.
Shade is decent in the hammocks and absent over the prairie rim.

## Trailheads and parking

**Boulware Springs Park** in Gainesville is the main western trailhead, with parking, water and
restrooms. **Hawthorne** has a trailhead at the eastern end. There are additional access points at
the Prairie Creek and La Chua areas.

Boulware Springs fills at weekends. Arrive early, or start from Hawthorne and ride toward the
prairie instead.

## Why an e-bike suits this trail

Two reasons. Sixteen miles one way is a 32-mile round trip with real climbing at the Gainesville end,
which is a genuine day on a pedal bike. And this is a trail where you want to stop constantly - the
overlooks, the boardwalks, the wildlife - and assist means the stopping does not cost you the ride
home.

North Florida is humid and hot from May to September. Ride early.

Class 1 and Class 2 are standard rentals and are generally accepted on Florida's state trails; Class
3 rules vary by managing authority. See our [e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/)
and the [Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/).

## Practical notes

- **Water** at Boulware Springs and Hawthorne, not reliably in between.
- **The La Chua Trail** off the prairie is walking only, and worth doing on foot if you have time.
- **Do not approach the wildlife.** Bison and horses are wild animals with no interest in you.
- **Ticks and mosquitoes** in the hammock sections. Repellent helps.
- **Rentals** are in Gainesville, a short drive from the Boulware Springs trailhead.

## FAQs

### How long is the Gainesville-Hawthorne Trail?

Sixteen miles one way, from Boulware Springs Park in Gainesville to the town of Hawthorne. A full
out-and-back is 32 miles.

### Where does the Gainesville-Hawthorne Trail start?

At Boulware Springs Park on the south-east edge of Gainesville, which has parking, water and
restrooms. The eastern end is at the Hawthorne trailhead.

### Can you see bison on the Gainesville-Hawthorne Trail?

Paynes Prairie Preserve carries free-ranging bison and wild horses, and both are sometimes visible
from the trail and its overlooks. Sightings are not guaranteed. Never approach them.

### Are there alligators on the Gainesville-Hawthorne Trail?

Yes, in numbers, particularly near the prairie and the La Chua area. Alligators on or beside the
path are common. Stop, give them space, and do not ride close past one.

### Is the Gainesville-Hawthorne Trail hilly?

By Florida standards, yes. There are short genuine climbs out of Boulware Springs and through the
hammock country. Nothing long, but noticeable.

### Can you ride an e-bike on the Gainesville-Hawthorne Trail?

Yes. Class 1 and Class 2 are the usual rentals and are generally accepted on Florida state trails.
Confirm the position on Class 3 with Florida State Parks or your rental shop.

### Where can I rent an e-bike in Gainesville?

Gainesville has rental shops within a short drive of the Boulware Springs trailhead. Book ahead in
term time, when the student market keeps stock tight.

## The short version

Park at Boulware Springs, ride south-east along the prairie rim, and take every overlook. It is the
best wildlife riding on pavement in Florida, and the one trail in the state where the reason to slow
down is not the heat.
