The Amelia Island Trail is the island's car-free ride: a paved multi-use path of a little over 7
miles that runs south from Peters Point Beachfront Park in Fernandina Beach, alongside A1A (First
Coast Highway), to the bridge over Nassau Sound at the island's southern tip, where it connects to
Big Talbot Island. It opened on May 11, 2013, is maintained by Nassau County and is supported by the
volunteer [Friends of the Amelia Island Trail](http://www.ameliaislandtrail.org/about.html).

Most of the route sits back from the highway under a tree canopy, so it is shaded and sheltered from
the ocean wind — the reason it is popular with families and with riders who would rather not share
a bike lane with A1A traffic. Shops, restaurants and beach accesses sit close to the path along the
way.

The trail is flat. An e-bike here is less about climbing than about stretching the ride: a full
out-and-back is roughly 14 to 15 miles, and with a rental you can add the Amelia River To Sea Trail,
downtown Fernandina or Fort Clinch on the same battery. Keep the speed down, though — **10 mph is the
posted limit for every bike on the trail** (details below).

## Route at a glance

Trailhead parking is listed at four parks. From north to south:

- **Peters Point Beachfront Park (1974 S Fletcher Ave, Fernandina Beach).** The North Trailhead, a free Nassau County beach park. Start here if you want the full trail and a beach stop at the end.
- **South End Beach Access.** Mid-trail parking near the southern beach accesses, handy for a shorter ride.
- **Amelia Island State Park (9550 1st Coast Hwy).** The South Trailhead, at the southern tip of the island. Florida State Parks lists the park as open **8 a.m. to sunset** with a **$2 per person** fee paid at an honor box (correct change required); the Friends of the Amelia Island Trail note no entry fee for cyclists riding through on the trail. Beach parking is capped at 300 people at a time, so the lot can close to new visitors on busy days.
- **Big Talbot Island State Park.** Across Nassau Sound, for riders coming up from the Jacksonville side.

## The rides worth doing

**Peters Point to the state park and back (about 14 to 15 miles).** The classic: shaded path
and Nassau Sound as the turnaround. It is a relaxed morning on Class 1
or Class 2 assist.

**Add the Amelia River To Sea Trail (ARTS).** Nassau County's second paved trail runs about 3 miles
between Crane Island Park on the Amelia River and Beach Access 30 at the east end of Simmons Road on
South Fletcher Avenue — river to ocean across the island. It opened on September 23, 2019. Parking
is at the Fernandina Beach Athletic Complex at the south end of Bailey Road and at Beach Access 30.

**Keep going south.** The Friends of the Amelia Island Trail note that the trail continues across
Nassau Sound as the Timucuan Trail, which Florida State Parks is building in phases through the
Talbot Islands, and that the corridor is part of the
[East Coast Greenway](https://www.greenway.org/). Check current conditions before you plan on it.

**North to downtown and Fort Clinch.** The trail ends at Peters Point; from there to historic
downtown Fernandina Beach and Fort Clinch State Park at the north end you are on city streets and
bike lanes, where the city's own rules apply (below).

## Rules on the trail

- **E-bikes are allowed.** Florida law gives an e-bike the same rights and duties as a bicycle and lets it use bicycle and shared-use paths, while letting the agency in charge of a path restrict e-bikes ([section 316.20655](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.20655)). Nassau County's trail rules, as revised by a 2021 county ordinance, welcome **Class 1, 2 and 3 e-bikes** on the Amelia Island Trail and the ARTS Trail, according to the [Friends of the Amelia Island Trail](http://www.ameliaislandtrail.org/about.html).
- **10 mph maximum** for all bicycles, posted at both trailheads and along the route.
- **No golf carts or other motor vehicles.** The only exceptions are electric ADA mobility devices up to 10 mph and official trail vehicles. No horses.
- **Keep right, warn before passing** (ring a bell or say "passing on your left") and step off the trail when you stop.
- **Yield to pedestrians.** No racing or pace lines.
- **Helmets** are required for riders under 16 under [Florida law](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.2065) and recommended for everyone.
- **Pets** on a leash under 6 feet; no alcohol or glass. Groups of more than 20 need Nassau County approval.
- **Watch for crossing vehicle traffic.** The trail crosses A1A at the S. Fletcher / Buccaneer Trail roundabout and again south of Quattlefield Road, and passes many driveways and side roads.

### In Fernandina Beach city limits

North of the county trail, the City of Fernandina Beach adopted
[Ordinance 2026-08](https://fernandinabeachfl.new.swagit.com/videos/388445) on personal mobility
devices; commissioners approved it unanimously on third reading in July 2026, as
[reported by the Fernandina Observer](https://www.fernandinaobserver.org/stories/how-fast-can-you-ride-an-e-bike-in-fernandina-beach-new-rules-set-the-limits,112934).
As reported:

- **25 mph** maximum for bicycles and e-bikes on city roadways (or the posted limit if lower).
- **10 mph** on trail networks, multi-use paths and city-owned beaches, where pedal assist is allowed.
- **Human power only on sidewalks** and unpaved parts of the public right-of-way, still at 10 mph or less.
- Riding is **not allowed in city recreational parks** unless signs say otherwise.
- Violations can bring a citation, with a city safety course or a $50 fine.

## FAQs

### How long is the Amelia Island Trail?

A little over 7 miles one way, from Peters Point Beachfront Park in Fernandina Beach to the A1A
bridge over Nassau Sound at the south end of the island. Out and back is roughly 14 to 15 miles.

### Are e-bikes allowed on the Amelia Island Trail?

Yes. Nassau County's trail rules allow Class 1, 2 and 3 e-bikes on the Amelia Island Trail and the
Amelia River To Sea Trail, with a 10 mph limit for all bicycles. Golf carts and other motor vehicles
are not allowed.

### Where should I park?

Peters Point Beachfront Park (free) for the north end, or Amelia Island State Park ($2 per person,
8 a.m. to sunset) for the south end. South End Beach Access and Big Talbot Island State Park also
have trail parking.

### Can I ride a rental e-bike on the beach?

Riptide and The Beach Store & More both tell renters their e-bikes are for roads, bike lanes and
paths, not the sand. Fernandina Beach's 2026 ordinance allows bikes on city-owned beaches at up to
10 mph, but follow your rental agreement and any posted signs.

### Is the trail shaded?

Mostly. Much of the route runs under a tree canopy beside A1A, which keeps it cooler and out of the
wind. Carry water anyway, especially on summer afternoons.

## The short version

Rent in Fernandina Beach (or have the bike delivered), start at Peters Point, keep it at 10 mph, ring
before you pass, and turn around at Nassau Sound. Add the ARTS Trail for a river-to-sea detour, and
ride downtown on city streets at no more than 25 mph.
