The Jacksonville-Baldwin Rail Trail is 14.5 miles of paved path running west from Imeson Road on
Jacksonville's west side out to the small town of Baldwin, on the corridor of the old Seaboard
Coast Line railroad.

It is the best trail ride inside Duval County: flat, straight, well surfaced, and shaded for far
more of its length than most Florida rail trails manage.

## The route

The eastern trailhead is on Imeson Road. From there the trail runs almost due west through pine
woods, wetland and rural Duval County, with a handful of road crossings, finishing at the Baldwin
trailhead at the western end.

Alongside the asphalt for most of the route runs a separate unpaved path for horses. Riders should
stay on the paved side and give horses a wide, slow, announced pass where the two paths meet.

## What it is like to ride

Straight, quiet and green. The corridor is enclosed by trees for long stretches, which means genuine
shade - the reason this trail is rideable in a Jacksonville summer when the beach paths are not.

There is very little at either end and nothing in the middle: a couple of road crossings, the
occasional bench, and a lot of pine. Baldwin has a small town centre with somewhere to get a drink,
which makes it a natural turnaround.

## Trailheads and parking

Two main trailheads: **Imeson Road** at the eastern end and **Baldwin** at the western end. Both have
free parking, and both are quiet on weekdays. Camp Milton Historic Preserve sits near the trail
partway along and is worth the short stop.

For a first ride, park at Imeson Road, ride west, and turn around wherever suits - the trail's
character does not change much, so there is no section you are missing out on.

## Surface, shade and conditions

- **Asphalt** in good condition for the full length.
- **Real shade** for much of the route, which is unusual and valuable in north Florida.
- **Few road crossings**, mostly rural and well sighted.
- **Standing water** in low spots after heavy rain.
- **Mosquitoes** at dawn and dusk in the wetter sections from May to October.

Jacksonville summers are humid rather than merely hot. The shade helps, but an early start still
helps more.

## Why an e-bike suits this trail

Fourteen and a half miles one way is a 29-mile round trip. That is a solid day on a pedal bike and a
comfortable morning with assist, which is what makes riding out to Baldwin and back a realistic plan
rather than an ambition. It also means you can ride the trail and still have the legs for the beach
in the afternoon.

Class 1 and Class 2 are what Jacksonville shops rent and what the city's shared-use paths commonly
allow; Class 3 rules are set locally. See our [e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/)
and the [Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/).

## Practical notes

- **Water** is at the trailheads, not between them. Carry it.
- **Horses** use the parallel path. Slow down, call out, and pass wide.
- **Baldwin** at the western end is the only place on the route to buy anything.
- **Rentals** are in Jacksonville and at the beaches, not on the trail. Several shops deliver.

## FAQs

### How long is the Jacksonville-Baldwin Rail Trail?

14.5 miles one way, from Imeson Road on Jacksonville's west side to Baldwin. An out-and-back is about
29 miles.

### Where does the Jacksonville-Baldwin Rail Trail start?

The eastern trailhead is on Imeson Road in Jacksonville; the western end is in the town of Baldwin.
Both have free parking.

### Is the Jacksonville-Baldwin Rail Trail shaded?

Yes, for much of its length - more than most Florida rail trails. That makes it one of the better
summer options on the First Coast.

### Are horses allowed on the Jacksonville-Baldwin Rail Trail?

Yes. A separate unpaved path runs parallel to the asphalt for horse riders. Cyclists should stay on
the paved trail and pass horses slowly and widely.

### Can you ride an e-bike on the Jacksonville-Baldwin Rail Trail?

E-bikes are commonly ridden here. Class 1 and Class 2 are the usual rentals and the classes generally
accepted on the city's shared-use paths; confirm Class 3 with the City of Jacksonville.

### Where can I rent an e-bike in Jacksonville for this trail?

Jacksonville and Jacksonville Beach have the most shops on the First Coast, with more in Ponte Vedra
Beach. Nothing rents at the trailhead, so plan on collection or delivery.

## The short version

Park at Imeson Road, ride west toward Baldwin under the pines, and turn round when you feel like it.
It is the most straightforward good ride in Jacksonville, and the shade makes it work in months when
the coast does not.
