The Lake Okeechobee Scenic Trail - the LOST - runs about 110 miles around Florida's great inland
lake, mostly along the crest of the Herbert Hoover Dike, the earth embankment the Army Corps of
Engineers built after the 1928 hurricane.

Roughly two-thirds of the loop is paved on top of the dike. The rest is unpaved dike, levee road or
on-road detour. It is part of the Florida National Scenic Trail, and it is unlike anything else you
can ride in this state.

## What it is actually like

You are riding on top of an embankment thirty-odd feet above the surrounding country, with the lake
on one side - often out of sight behind a marsh fringe - and sugar cane, pasture and canals on the
other. There is no shade. There is no shelter. There is very little between the small towns on the
rim.

It is exposed, it is windy, and it is spectacular in a way that has nothing to do with scenery in
the postcard sense. Sunrise and sunset from the dike are the reason people come back.

## The towns on the rim

The loop passes through or beside Okeechobee, Clewiston, Moore Haven, Belle Glade, Pahokee, South
Bay, Port Mayaca and Lakeport. These are the resupply points and the practical section boundaries.

Distances between them run from about 10 to 25 miles. Plan your day around them rather than around
mileage.

## Sections worth riding

**Port Mayaca to Pahokee (about 12 miles).** Paved, on the eastern rim, with good lake access and the
easiest approach from the Treasure Coast or the Palm Beaches.

**Okeechobee to Henry Creek and the northern rim.** Paved dike with wide views and the most
convenient parking on the loop.

**Clewiston to Moore Haven (about 15 miles).** The southern rim, cane country, and the section that
feels most like Old Florida agriculture.

Check with the South Florida Water Management District and the Army Corps of Engineers for current
access: dike rehabilitation work has closed sections at various times, and closures can move.

## Wind and heat: the real difficulty

The riding is flat. The difficulty is entirely environmental:

- **Wind** across the open lake is relentless and often 15-20mph. A tailwind out becomes a wall on
  the way back.
- **No shade** anywhere on the dike. None.
- **Water** only in the towns.
- **Storms** are visible for twenty miles and arrive quickly, with no shelter on the embankment.
- **Sun exposure** is total, including reflection off the water and the pale dike surface.

This is the Florida trail most likely to punish a casual approach. Ride early, carry more water than
you think, and always plan the return leg into the wind rather than out of it.

## Why an e-bike suits this trail

More than on any other Florida trail. The wind here is the terrain, and assist is what flattens it.
A 25-mile section that would be a hard, gritty day on a pedal bike into a 20mph headwind becomes a
pleasant morning - and, importantly, becomes a ride you can abandon and reverse without being stranded
30 miles from the car.

For anyone attempting the full 110-mile loop, e-bikes turn a three or four-day trip into a two-day
one, with charging in the rim towns as the planning constraint.

Class 1 and Class 2 are the standard rentals. Rules on the dike are set by its managing agencies
rather than by the state - see our [e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/) and the
[Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/), and check current access before you go.

## Practical notes

- **Check closures first.** Dike works have closed sections in the past and access can change.
- **Charge in the towns.** There is nothing on the dike.
- **Take real sun protection**: long sleeves, a hat under the helmet, and more sunscreen than seems
  reasonable.
- **Alligators** are everywhere in the canals and the rim marsh. Do not go down to the water.
- **Rentals** are not on the lake. Stuart, Port St. Lucie and the Palm Beaches are the nearest
  clusters; most riders bring their own bikes or arrange delivery.

## FAQs

### How long is the Lake Okeechobee Scenic Trail?

About 110 miles for the full loop around the lake, most of it on the crest of the Herbert Hoover
Dike.

### Is the Lake Okeechobee Scenic Trail paved?

Roughly two-thirds of it. The rest is unpaved dike surface, levee road, or on-road detour around
sections that are closed or were never surfaced.

### How long does it take to ride around Lake Okeechobee?

Three to four days is typical on a pedal bike, staying in the rim towns. On e-bikes, two days is
realistic if you can charge overnight.

### Is there shade on the Lake Okeechobee Scenic Trail?

No. The dike is completely exposed for its whole length. This is the single most important thing to
plan around.

### Can you see the lake from the trail?

Sometimes. The dike is set back behind a marsh fringe in many places, so the open water comes and
goes. The eastern and northern rims give the best sustained views.

### Can you ride an e-bike on the Lake Okeechobee Scenic Trail?

E-bikes are ridden on the dike and are well suited to it. Access and rules are set by the Army Corps
of Engineers and the South Florida Water Management District rather than the state; check current
conditions and any closures before you ride.

### Where can I rent an e-bike near Lake Okeechobee?

There is very little rental provision on the lake itself. Stuart, Port St. Lucie, Okeechobee and the
Palm Beaches are the nearest options, and delivery is the usual arrangement.

## The short version

Do not treat this as a casual ride. Pick one paved section - Port Mayaca to Pahokee, or the northern
rim out of Okeechobee - ride out into the wind, come back with it, and be off the dike before the
afternoon heat. It is the most exposed and most memorable riding in Florida.
