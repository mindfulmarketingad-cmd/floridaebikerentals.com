The Palatka-to-St. Augustine State Trail is Northeast Florida's finished paved link between the
outskirts of St. Augustine and East Palatka: about 19 miles of 12-foot asphalt through St. Johns and
Putnam counties, mostly parallel to State Road 207. Florida State Parks manages the corridor as a
multi-use rail trail through farmland, scrub and small towns — Elkton, Armstrong, Hastings and East
Palatka — with trailheads that have parking and restrooms on the St. Johns County end.

It does **not** dump you into downtown St. Augustine. The eastern tip dead-ends at the railroad
tracks just past Vermont Boulevard; the useful starts are Vermont Heights, Armstrong Park and Cora
C. Harrison Preserve. On the west end, East River Road marks the state-trail terminus near East
Palatka, with a short connector into Palatka (a designated Florida Trail Town) if you plan ahead.

An e-bike makes the full out-and-back (roughly 38 miles from Vermont Heights) a comfortable day
instead of a grind, and Class 1 or Class 2 assist is plenty on the flat pavement. November through
April is the easy season; summer heat and afternoon storms build quickly over the open farm stretches.

## Route at a glance

Florida State Parks lists the trailheads from east to west. Mileages below are the park's own
round-trip figures from each eastern start to the Putnam County end and back:

- **Vermont Blvd. Trailhead (GPS 29.81569, -81.40269).** Off Vermont Boulevard in St. Johns County.
  Kiosk and roadside parking only. Farthest east access; the trail continues about a mile east and
  dead-ends at the railroad tracks. From here west to East River Road and back is about 35 miles.
- **Vermont Heights Trailhead (GPS 29.80313, -81.41137).** About a mile west of Vermont Boulevard.
  Managed by St. Johns County Parks and Recreation: kiosk, parking, restroom and picnic area. The
  usual eastern start — about 34 miles round-trip to East River Road.
- **Armstrong Park (GPS 29.76242, -81.44736).** St. Johns County park with restrooms, parking, picnic
  area and a playground. About 5 miles from Vermont Boulevard and about 12 miles from the Putnam
  County end. An interpretive exhibit near the trail covers Armstrong's African-American community
  history.
- **Cora C. Harrison Preserve (GPS 29.71619, -81.51528).** St. Johns County trailhead with restroom,
  parking and a picnic area. About 9 miles from Vermont Boulevard and about 7 miles from East River
  Road — a good mid-trail base for a shorter out-and-back.
- **East River Road Trailhead (GPS 29.66153, -81.60081).** Putnam County end of the state trail:
  three roadside parking spaces and a map kiosk. Downtown Palatka is a short planned connector past
  this point, not part of the 19-mile state-trail pavement itself.

Five map kiosks sit along the corridor. Two sections cross U.S. 207 — use the signals. Cross traffic
at other intersections does not stop for trail users; you do.

## The sections worth riding

**Vermont Heights to Armstrong Park and back (about 10 miles).** The pick for a first ride from the
St. Augustine side: parking and restrooms at both ends, a mix of roadside and wooded stretches, and
enough distance to feel the trail without committing to Hastings.

**Vermont Heights through Hastings to Cora C. Harrison and back.** Adds farmland, the Armstrong
history stop and a quieter mid-corridor feel. Turn around at Harrison if you want roughly an 18-mile
day with facilities at both ends.

**Armstrong or Harrison to East River Road and back.** The western half toward East Palatka, with
fewer services between trailheads. Useful if you are staging from the Putnam County end or combining
the ride with downtown Palatka after the East River Road terminus.

**The whole thing, one way or out-and-back.** Nineteen miles one way is an easy e-bike morning on
moderate assist. An out-and-back of about 34–35 miles from Vermont Heights fits most rental batteries
if you start full and keep the assist modest. Ask the shop what range to expect, and carry more water
than the distance suggests — shade and services thin out between the towns.

## Rules on the trail

- **E-bikes are allowed.** Florida State Parks says bicycles, including electric bicycles, may use
  trails designated for bicycle use, consistent with Section 316.20655 of Florida law. The park's
  [experiences page](https://www.floridastateparks.org/parks-and-trails/palatka-st-augustine-state-trail/experiences-amenities)
  is the current word.
- **Obey posted speed limits** and ride at a safe speed. Drop the assist near walkers; our
  [e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/) explains what each class can do.
- **Yield and stop.** Be respectful of hikers and other users. Cross traffic at road crossings does
  not stop — you do. Use the signals at the U.S. 207 crossings.
- **Hours.** Florida State Parks lists the trail at 8 a.m. to sunset, 365 days a year, with **no
  fee**.
- **Dogs are welcome** on a leash no longer than six feet. Walk dogs near the edge of the trail and
  pick up after them.
- **Helmets** are recommended for everyone. Florida law requires them for riders under 16.

Our [Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/) covers the rest of the state rules. For
downtown St. Augustine riding (Bridge of Lions, Anastasia Island and winter Nights of Lights
restrictions), see our [Nights of Lights by bike guide](/blog/st-augustine-nights-of-lights-by-bike/)
and the [St. Augustine](/cities/st-augustine/) page — those are separate from this rail trail.

## Renting for the Palatka-to-St. Augustine State Trail

There is no rental desk at the trailheads. Most visitors rent in St. Augustine or St. Augustine Beach
and drive to Vermont Heights or Armstrong; a Palatka listing puts you nearer the west end. Confirm
with the shop that the bike can leave their local area and that you have enough range for your plan.

On the beach side, **Island Life Bikes** at 105 A1A Beach Blvd lists bicycle rentals (4.9 stars /
1,304 reviews) and **DRIFTERS Rentals** at 645 A1A Beach Blvd is a bike rental service (5.0 stars /
373 reviews). **St. Augustine AC Electric Bike Rentals** at 510 B St on St. Augustine Beach is listed
as an e-bike rental shop with no Google reviews yet, so call ahead. Details:

and scooters (4.4 stars / 280 reviews), and **Solano Cycle of St. Augustine** at 100 San Marco Ave

## FAQs

### How long is the Palatka-to-St. Augustine State Trail?

About 19 miles one way of paved state trail from the Vermont Boulevard / Vermont Heights area west
to East River Road near East Palatka. Florida State Parks describes it as a 12-foot-wide multi-use
corridor through St. Johns and Putnam counties.

### Are e-bikes allowed on the Palatka-to-St. Augustine State Trail?

Yes. Florida State Parks allows electric bicycles on trails designated for bicycle use, consistent
with Section 316.20655 of Florida law. Ride at a safe speed and yield to other trail users.

### Does the trail go into downtown St. Augustine?

No. The eastern tip dead-ends at the railroad tracks past Vermont Boulevard. Rent in town or on the
beach, then drive to Vermont Heights, Armstrong Park or Cora C. Harrison Preserve to start the paved
corridor.

### Where is the best place to start?

Vermont Heights for parking, restrooms and the full eastern approach. Armstrong Park if you want
facilities and a slightly shorter day. Cora C. Harrison Preserve for a mid-trail start. East River
Road if you are coming from the Palatka side.

### What are the trail's hours, and is there a fee?

Florida State Parks lists the trail 8 a.m. to sunset every day with no fee.

### Can you ride the whole trail in a day on an e-bike?

One way, easily. An out-and-back of about 34–35 miles from Vermont Heights is a comfortable day on
most rental e-bikes if you start with a full charge. Confirm range with the shop before you commit.

## The short version

Rent in St. Augustine, drive to Vermont Heights after 8 a.m., ride west at least as far as Armstrong
or Hastings, stop for every road crossing, and carry more water than the farm stretches suggest. It
is the paved Northeast Florida day ride between the First Coast and Palatka, and late autumn through
spring is the time to do it.

Confirm opening hours with the shop before you drive over.
