Shark Valley is the Everglades ride people remember: a flat, paved 15-mile loop through sawgrass
prairie, with alligators and wading birds along the canal and an observation tower at the halfway
point. It sits at the Shark Valley entrance to Everglades National Park, about an hour west of Miami
on the Tamiami Trail (U.S. 41 / SW 8th Street). You drive to the visitor entrance; this is not a ride
you start on South Beach and continue into the park.

The National Park Service describes the Shark Valley Tram Road as roughly eight feet wide, paved and
free of rough terrain, and suitable for any type of bicycle. The observation tower, at the midpoint,
is the highest point you can reach on foot in the park, with a view across the River of Grass on a
clear day.

An e-bike makes the full loop a comfortable morning instead of a grind in the heat, which is why it
shows up in our [best Florida e-bike rides](/blog/best-ebike-rides-florida/). The catch is practical:
the park allows qualifying e-bikes, but the on-site concession rents regular single-gear bicycles,
not e-bikes. Most visitors either rent those bikes at the entrance or bring an e-bike they already
have (or rented in Miami before the drive).

November through April is the busy season, when wildlife viewing is usually better and parking is
harder. Summer is hotter, with almost no shade on the road and afternoon thunderstorms that build
quickly.

## Route at a glance

The loop starts behind the Shark Valley Visitor Center. The Park Service recommends riding it
counterclockwise, from the west side of the parking area:

- **West road to the observation tower (about 7 miles).** A long, straight paved road that follows a
  canal south. This is the stretch where you are most likely to see alligators, turtles, snakes and
  wading birds along the bank. The [Shark Valley Observation Tower](https://www.nps.gov/ever/planyourvisit/svdirections.htm)
  sits at the halfway point.
- **The tower.** Walk up for the view over the sawgrass. The Park Service notes a short trail through
  a tropical hardwood hammock below the tower, and a water fountain here, about seven miles from the
  parking lot. Do not count on that fountain as your only water.
- **East return to the visitor center (about 8 miles).** A more winding road back, with wider vistas
  and borrow-pit lakes where animals often show up. Together the two legs make the 15-mile loop.

Two short **walking** trails leave the tram road near the visitor center. Leave the bike secured and
go on foot: the Bobcat Boardwalk (about 0.2 miles, an accessible boardwalk through slough and hammock)
and the Otter Cave Hammock Trail (about 0.3 miles of rough limestone, starting about half a mile
behind the visitor center; it can flood in summer). Bikes stay on the tram road.

Private vehicles are not allowed beyond the entrance road and parking lot. The only ways to the tower
are by bicycle, on foot or on the concession's tram.

## The sections worth riding

**The full counterclockwise loop (15 miles).** The ride to come for. The Park Service says to plan at
least two to three hours for the loop itself; stops for wildlife and the tower can stretch that to a
half day. The on-site concession describes a typical ride as two to three hours with plenty of
stops. There is very little shade, so start early.

**The west road to the tower and back (about 14 miles).** If you want the canal wildlife and the
tower without committing to the winding return, ride the straight west road to the tower and
retrace it. You miss the borrow-pit lakes on the east side, but you still get the park's headline
view.

**A shorter walk-and-roll from the visitor center.** Families who do not want the full distance can
ride or walk the first stretch of the tram road, add the Bobcat Boardwalk, and turn around when they
have had enough. The tram, run by the same concession that rents bikes, is the no-pedaling way to
see the whole loop and the tower.

Whatever you ride, give wildlife room. The Park Service asks visitors to stay 15 feet (5 meters)
from animals and makes it illegal to feed, touch or harass them. Alligators use the roadway verge;
wait and go around at a distance rather than crowding them.

## Rules on the trail

These are National Park Service rules for Everglades National Park, not Florida State Parks rules.
Section 316.20655 of Florida law is the statewide e-bike framework; it does not replace the
superintendent's rules inside the park.

- **E-bikes are allowed** on the Shark Valley Tram Road if they have fully operable pedals and an
  electric motor of less than 750 watts (1 horsepower). That is the Park Service's definition on its
  [biking page](https://www.nps.gov/ever/planyourvisit/biking.htm) and its
  [Shark Valley biking page](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/biking-at-shark-valley.htm). The speed
  limit on the tram road is **15 miles per hour**. Other bicycle trails in the park are posted at 10
  miles per hour. Fully electric bikes that do not require pedaling are treated like motor vehicles:
  they belong on public roadways, not on the tram road. Our
  [e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/) explains Class 1, 2 and 3; whatever you
  bring, stay under the posted 15 mph and yield to people on foot.
- **Stop for the tram and for authorized park vehicles.** They share the same narrow road.
- **Roller blades, skateboards, longboards, scooters (except mobility scooters) and other
  motorized vehicles are not allowed** on the tram/bicycle trail. E-bikes that meet the definition
  above are the exception.
- **Helmets** are required for riders under 16, which matches Florida law and is restated by the
  Park Service at Shark Valley. Helmets are a good idea for everyone; the concession offers them
  with its rental bikes.
- **Pets** (other than service animals) are not allowed on the tram road. They may stay in the
  parking lot only. The Park Service is blunt about why: alligators take prey about the size of a
  dog.
- **No fishing** at Shark Valley, and do not assume there will be shade or services once you leave
  the visitor center.

Our [Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/) covers the statewide rules that apply
once you are back on ordinary roads and paths around Miami.

## Hours, parking and fees

- **Entrance gate.** The Shark Valley area is open daily from 8:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. Vehicles cannot
  enter after 6:00 p.m. The Park Service says you may bike at Shark Valley at any time of day; if
  you arrive or leave outside the gate hours, you need to park along Highway 41 and enter on foot or
  by bicycle.
- **Parking.** The lot is small for the demand. The Park Service warns of long waits between 10 a.m.
  and 3 p.m., and that the lot may run as one car in, one car out. During the peak months, typically
  December into April, it says parking is extremely limited and usually fills by 10 a.m. Arriving on
  a weekday, before 10 a.m. or after 3 p.m., is the advice on the
  [Shark Valley Visitor Center page](https://www.nps.gov/ever/planyourvisit/svdirections.htm), which
  also posts a phone line (305-221-8776) for current access conditions. The busy visitor season here
  runs from November through April.
- **Entrance fees.** Everglades National Park charges an entrance fee, separate from any bike rental
  or tram ticket. The park's
  [fees page](https://www.nps.gov/ever/planyourvisit/fees.htm) lists a private-vehicle pass at $35
  and a motorcycle pass at $30, each good for seven consecutive days at all park entrances; someone
  arriving by bicycle or on foot (age 16 or older) pays $20, and children 15 and under are free.
  America the Beautiful and other federal passes are accepted. The park does not take cash. Digital
  passes can be bought ahead of time. Non-U.S. residents should read that fees page before they go,
  because additional charges can apply unless they hold an annual or America the Beautiful pass.
- **Visitor center hours** vary by season and staffing. The park's
  [hours page](https://www.nps.gov/ever/planyourvisit/hours.htm) gives typical ranges (generally
  8 a.m. to 5 p.m. in the drier months and 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. in the wetter months) and tells you to
  confirm on the visitor center page or by calling the main park line at 305-242-7700.

Bring more water, sun protection and snacks than the mileage suggests. The Park Service says only
snacks and drinks are for sale through the concession, and it recommends packing a lunch. It also
warns that visitors who arrive by Uber, Lyft or taxi often cannot get a ride back; there is no other
public transportation to the site.

## Renting for Shark Valley

The on-site concession is
[Shark Valley Tram Tours](/cities/miami/shark-valley-tram-tours/) at 36000 SW 8th St, Miami, FL
33194. It is the authorized bike-rental and tram operator at this entrance
([sharkvalleytramtours.com](https://www.sharkvalleytramtours.com/)), and it is the shop in our
directory for this location (4.6 stars from 1,737 Google reviews). It also sells snacks and soft
drinks and runs the narrated tram around the same 15-mile loop.

Those rentals are **regular bicycles, not e-bikes**. The concession describes them as single-gear,
coaster-brake bikes with adjustable seats, plus 20-inch children's bikes and child seats for
younger riders who meet its size and age rules. Baskets and helmets are available. A driver's
license or ID is required. On its
[bike rental page](https://www.sharkvalleytramtours.com/product/daily-bike-rental/) it lists a
daily rate of $27 per bike; confirm the current price when you reserve, because published figures
have not always matched across park and concession pages.

Bike rentals begin at 8:30 a.m. and continue until 4 p.m., and every bike has to be back at the
rental office by 5 p.m. The Park Service recommends reserving for the winter months, especially on
weekends; leftover bikes are also rented first-come, first-served, and the concession says it holds
some bikes back for walk-up rentals even when the online allotment is sold out. Advanced online
rentals are described as available for pickup between 8:30 a.m. and noon.

If you specifically want an e-bike on the loop, you bring your own or rent one **before you drive
to the park**. There is no e-bike desk at Shark Valley, and there is no honest way to ride a rental
from South Beach to the visitor entrance: it is a highway drive of about an hour. Ask the shop
whether you may transport the bike that far. Many Miami Beach rentals are meant for riding away
from the counter, not for loading into a car.

## FAQs

### How long is the Shark Valley bike loop?

Fifteen miles, paved, starting and ending at the Shark Valley Visitor Center. The Park Service
describes about seven straight miles along the west canal to the observation tower and about eight
more winding miles back on the east side.

### Are e-bikes allowed at Shark Valley?

Yes, if they have fully operable pedals and a motor under 750 watts. The speed limit on the tram
road is 15 mph. Bikes that are fully electric and do not require pedaling are not allowed on that
road.

### Does Shark Valley rent e-bikes?

No. Shark Valley Tram Tours rents single-gear, coaster-brake bicycles, including children's bikes
and bikes with child seats. If you want an e-bike, bring one or rent it in Miami or Miami Beach
before you drive to the park.

### What are the hours and the entrance fee?

The Shark Valley entrance is open 8:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. daily. Bike rentals run from 8:30 a.m.
until 4 p.m. and must be returned by 5 p.m. The park charges a separate entrance fee (currently $35
per private vehicle or $20 per person arriving by bicycle or on foot if you are 16 or older, for
seven days); check the National Park Service
[fees page](https://www.nps.gov/ever/planyourvisit/fees.htm) for passes and any extra charges that
apply to you.

### Can I ride an e-bike from Miami Beach to Shark Valley?

No. Shark Valley is a drive west of Miami on the Tamiami Trail, not a continuation of the South
Beach bike lanes. Rent on site for a regular bike, or rent an e-bike in town only if the shop lets
you transport it to the park.

### When is the best time to go?

Early, especially from November through April, when the lot often fills mid-morning. The drier
winter months are also when wildlife is usually easier to see. In the hotter months, start as soon
as the gate opens, carry extra water and watch the afternoon storm forecast.

## The short version

Drive to the Shark Valley entrance early, pay the park fee, and ride the 15-mile paved loop
counterclockwise to the observation tower and back. Qualifying e-bikes are allowed at 15 mph; the
bikes rented on site are regular single-gear models, not e-bikes. Bring more water than you think,
give alligators a wide berth, and do not count on finding a parking space if you arrive at midday
in winter.

Confirm hours, fees and rental terms on the official pages before you go.
