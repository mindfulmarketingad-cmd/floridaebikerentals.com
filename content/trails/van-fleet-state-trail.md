The General James A. Van Fleet State Trail is the quietest long ride in this series: 29.2 miles of
8-foot-wide asphalt through the Green Swamp, with only one curve in the whole corridor. Florida State
Parks calls it one of Florida's most rural paved rail-trails, and that is exactly right. There are no
trail towns mid-route, no cafes, and almost no road noise once you leave Polk City.

It follows an old Seaboard railbed across Polk, Lake and Sumter counties, from Polk City north to
Mabel just off SR 50. Much of the western side of the northern half borders the Green Swamp and
Richloam wildlife management areas. The landscapes shift from ranch pasture to pine flatwoods, river
swamp and former citrus land. It is also a National Recreation Trail.

That remoteness is the point of the ride, and it is why an e-bike suits it. The distance is easy on
assist, but you will not find a spare battery, water or a mechanic between trailheads. Pack for the
day, start with a full charge, and treat each trailhead as a real supply stop.

November to April is the comfortable season. The Green Swamp holds heat and insects in summer, and
afternoon storms build fast with little cover between the four trailheads.

## Route at a glance

Mileage runs south to north. The four trailheads sit about ten miles apart, which makes half-day
out-and-backs easy to plan:

- **Polk City Trailhead (mile 0).** 7683 Berkley Road, Polk City. Parking, picnic pavilion, water and a restroom about half a mile north on the trail. Freedom Park is next door. This is the busiest start and the easiest one to reach from I-4. A paved connector around the lot leads under SR 33 onto the short Polk City Trail, which joins the Auburndale TECO Trail.
- **Green Pond Trailhead (mile 10).** 4903 Green Pond Road, Polk City. Parking, pavilion, water and restrooms. A good mid-trail base if you want options in both directions, and the closest trailhead to the three short bridges over the headwaters of the Withlacoochee River.
- **Bay Lake Trailhead (mile 20).** 7500 Bay Lake Road, Groveland. Parking, pavilion and restrooms. The most remote of the four; from here north the trail often forms the edge of the Withlacoochee State Forest.
- **Mabel Trailhead (mile 29.2).** 7981 County Road 772, Webster, just off SR 50. Parking, pavilion, water and restrooms. The northern end.

Florida State Parks notes that potable water is at Polk City, Green Pond and Mabel. Carry more than
you think you need between stops. An equestrian trail uses the 5-foot mowed shoulder on both sides of
the pavement; that shoulder is for horses, not bikes.

## The sections worth riding

**Green Pond to the bridges and back (about 6 to 8 miles).** The pick for a first ride. Start at Green
Pond, ride a couple of miles north into the Green Swamp Wilderness Preserve, cross the three short
bridges, and turn around. Wildlife viewing is strongest between Green Pond and Bay Lake.

**Polk City to Green Pond and back (about 20 miles).** The southern half, with the trail's only curve
around mile 5, a crossing at Deen Still Road and a gradual fade of road noise as you leave town. Use
this if you are coming from Lakeland or the I-4 corridor and want the simplest logistics.

**Bay Lake to Mabel and back (about 18 miles).** The shaded northern forest section. Services are
thinner here, so fill water at Mabel or Bay Lake before you start.

**The whole thing, one way.** 29.2 miles is a comfortable e-bike morning on moderate assist. The hard
part is getting back: there are no shops on the trail, so arrange a car shuttle between Polk City and
Mabel, or ride a section out and back. Ask your rental shop what range to expect before you commit to
the full length.

## Rules on the trail

- **E-bikes are allowed.** Florida State Parks says bicycles, including electric bicycles, may use trails designated for bicycle use, consistent with [section 316.20655](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.20655) of Florida law. [Florida State Parks](https://www.floridastateparks.org/learn/explore-bike) states the same rule. The park's [experiences page](https://www.floridastateparks.org/parks-and-trails/general-james-van-fleet-state-trail/experiences-amenities) is the current word.
- **Ride at a safe speed** and yield to other users. Cyclists yield to walkers, and everyone yields to horses on the shoulder. Keep right, pass on the left and give a clear warning when you pass. Our [e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/) explains what each class can do.
- **Hours are 8 a.m. to sunset**, 365 days a year, and there is **no fee**.
- **Dogs are welcome** on a leash no longer than six feet. Dog-friendly drinking fountains are at Polk City and Green Pond.
- **Hunting seasons** run in the Green Swamp and Richloam wildlife management areas on the western side of the northern half. The trail stays open; wear high-visibility clothing in season and give hunters space.
- **Helmets** are recommended for everyone. [Florida law](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.2065) requires them for riders and passengers under 16.

Our [Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/) covers the rest of the state rules.

## Renting for the Van Fleet State Trail

There is no rental desk at any trailhead. You rent in a nearby town and drive or ride to the trail,
so confirm with the shop that the bike can leave their local area.

The closest e-bike rental to the northern half is **EGO Depot** in Groveland, which lists e-bike and
e-trike rentals and names the Van Fleet among the trails it serves. Details are on the
[EGO Depot listing](/cities/groveland/ego-depot/).

For the Polk City (south) end, **LeRoy's Bikeworks** in Lakeland lists electric bikes and rentals.
**Trek Bicycle Store Clermont** and **Midpoint Rec** in Clermont list bicycle rentals if you are
starting from Bay Lake or combining the ride with Clermont's South Lake Trail. Ratings, hours and
directions are on our [Lakeland](/cities/lakeland/) and [Clermont](/cities/clermont/) pages, and the
nearest shops appear at the foot of this guide.

If you are already renting in Winter Garden for the [West Orange Trail](/trails/west-orange-trail/),
ask the shop about a second-day run to Polk City or Bay Lake before you assume the bike can travel
that far.

A comfortable Class 1 or Class 2 cruiser or commuter is the right bike for the pavement. Our
[commuter e-bike guide](/electric-bike-types/commuter-electric-bikes/) covers bikes built for long
days. For a multi-day stay, compare [daily and weekly rates](/costs/daily-vs-weekly-ebike-rental-rates/)
and run through our [pre-rental checklist](/blog/ebike-rental-checklist/) before you leave the lot.

## FAQs

### How long is the Van Fleet State Trail?

29.2 miles from the Polk City Trailhead to the Mabel Trailhead, with trailheads at roughly miles 0,
10, 20 and 29.2.

### Are e-bikes allowed on the Van Fleet State Trail?

Yes. Florida State Parks allows electric bicycles on trails designated for bicycle use, consistent
with [section 316.20655](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.20655) of Florida law. Ride at a safe speed and yield to walkers and horses.

### Where is the best place to start the Van Fleet State Trail?

Green Pond for the swamp and bridges, Polk City if you are coming from I-4 or Lakeland, and Bay Lake
or Mabel for the quieter forest end.

### What are the trail's hours, and is there a fee?

The trail is open 8 a.m. to sunset every day, and there is no fee.

### Is there parking on the Van Fleet State Trail?

Yes. All four trailheads — Polk City, Green Pond, Bay Lake and Mabel — have parking and picnic
pavilions. Restrooms are at Green Pond, Bay Lake and Mabel, plus about half a mile north of Polk City.

### Can you ride the whole trail in a day on an e-bike?

One way, yes, if your battery has the range and you arrange a shuttle back. An out-and-back of nearly
60 miles is beyond most rental e-bikes, so most riders pick one section.

## The short version

Rent near Groveland or Lakeland, start after 8 a.m. at Green Pond, ride north to the bridges and
back, and carry more water than the distance suggests. It is the flattest, quietest long paved ride
in Central Florida, and late autumn through spring is the time to do it.

Confirm opening hours with the shop before you drive over.
