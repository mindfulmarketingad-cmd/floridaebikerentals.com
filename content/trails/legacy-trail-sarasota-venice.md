The Legacy Trail is Southwest Florida's long-distance ride: 18.5 miles of paved rail trail from
Fruitville Road on the edge of downtown Sarasota south through Osprey and Nokomis to the Historic
Venice Train Depot. Add the Venetian Waterway Park paths at the Venice end and the paved network runs
to about 20 miles.

It follows the old Seaboard Air Line rail corridor, the line that once carried passengers to Venice
and, for three decades, the Ringling circus to its winter home. That history is why it is so easy to
ride: long straight runs, gentle grades and almost no elevation at all. The route map below climbs
about 85 feet over its whole length.

## Route at a glance

The trail is flat, paved and continuous. Since the northern extension opened in 2022 it no longer
stops in the suburbs: it runs right up past Payne Park to Fruitville Road, a short ride from downtown
Sarasota. Sarasota County lists 16 trailheads along it, many with parking, restrooms, water and
picnic shelters.

Mileage runs from south to north, starting at zero at the Venice Train Depot. The posts along the
trail follow the railroad's original numbering, so use the mile figures below rather than the
markers if the two disagree.

On an e-bike the whole trail is a comfortable one-way morning, and an out-and-back of about 37 miles
is realistic on a full battery for most riders on moderate assist. Most people ride a section instead,
using a trailhead as a base.

## Trailheads by mile

Mile figures are from the Friends of the Legacy Trail mile log, measured from the Venice Train Depot.

- **Venice Train Depot (mile 0).** The southern end and the most scenic start, with parking, a bike
  repair station and the connection onto the Venetian Waterway Park paths.
- **Patriots Park (0.8) and Nokomis Community Park (1.0).** Two close-together access points on the
  north side of Venice.
- **Nokomis Riverview Park (1.5) and Laurel Park (2.4).** Quieter neighbourhood trailheads, with the
  Dona Bay bridge between them.
- **Oscar Scherer State Park access (5.3).** A side trip into the park's scrub and flatwoods. The
  park charges an entrance fee, cyclists included. Just south of it the trail crosses an original
  railroad trestle.
- **Osprey Junction (6.7).** A practical mid-trail trailhead with parking.
- **McIntosh Road access (9.7) and Culverhouse Nature Park (10.5).** The Palmer Ranch section, with
  shaded paths and a community garden at Culverhouse.
- **Ashton Road (12.5).** Bike repair station.
- **Sarasota Springs Trailhead, Webber Street (14.7).** Parking and a repair station, and the start of
  the busier northern section.
- **Pompano Trailhead (16.9).** Next to the Babe Ruth baseball complex, with a repair station.
- **Payne Park (18.1) to Fruitville Road (18.5).** The northern gateway, a park with restrooms and
  the link into downtown.

## The sections worth riding

**Venice to Osprey Junction and back (about 13 miles).** The pick for a first ride. Start at the
train depot, cross the bridges through Nokomis, pass Oscar Scherer State Park and the old trestle, and
turn around at Osprey Junction. It is the most natural-feeling stretch of the trail.

**Culverhouse to Payne Park (about 7.5 miles one way).** The northern extension, and the newest
part of the trail. More neighbourhoods and more crossings, but the busiest roads are bridged:
overpasses carry the trail over Clark Road and Bee Ridge Road. Ride it if you want to finish in
downtown Sarasota for lunch.

**The whole thing, one way.** 18.5 miles is an easy distance on assist. The problem is getting back,
so either ride out and back, or arrange with your rental shop for a pickup or a one-way drop if it
offers one. Ask before you book rather than assuming.

**Venetian Waterway Park.** At the Venice end the trail connects to the waterway paths along the
Intracoastal. The east segment is just under 4 miles, with water views most of the way, and is the
best way to stretch a Venice ride toward the beach.

## Rules on the trail

Sarasota County's rules are short and worth knowing before you ride a rented e-bike:

- **E-bikes are allowed.** Functional pedals are required, and no other motorised vehicles are
  permitted.
- **Speed limit 15 mph.** That is below the assisted top speed of a Class 2 or Class 3 e-bike, so set
  a lower assist level and leave it there. See our [e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/)
  for what each class can do.
- **Warn before passing, pass on the left, single file**, and give three feet of clearance.
- **Bicycles yield to everyone else** on the trail, walkers included.
- **Stop at road crossings** and use the crossing signals.
- **Hours are 6 a.m. to sunset** on the trail itself. Payne Park opens at 7 a.m.

Florida law requires helmets for riders under 16. Our [Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/)
covers the rest of the state rules.

## Shade, heat and timing

Shade is patchy. Some sections run under tree cover, particularly around the parks, but much of the
corridor is open and bakes from late spring to early autumn. From May to September, ride early: the
trail opens at 6 a.m., and the first three hours of the day are the best of it.

Winter is peak season here. It is the most comfortable time to ride, and also when rental shops are
busiest, so book bikes ahead from January to April. The trail is dog-friendly on a leash, and you
will meet plenty of dogs on the popular sections at weekends.

## Renting for the Legacy Trail

Most rental shops for the trail are in Sarasota and Venice, and several Sarasota shops sit within
easy reach of a trailhead. The full lists, with Google ratings, hours and directions, are on our
[Sarasota bike rentals](/find/ebike-rentals-in-sarasota/) and
[Venice bike rentals](/find/ebike-rentals-in-venice/) pages, and the shops nearest each end appear at
the foot of this guide.

A Class 1 or Class 2 cruiser is the right bike. A fat-tyre bike is extra weight for no benefit on a
paved trail, unless you also plan to ride sand elsewhere in your stay. If you are staying on Siesta
Key or Lido and want bikes for the week, ask about [delivery](/find/ebike-rentals-with-delivery-in-florida/)
and compare [daily and weekly rates](/costs/daily-vs-weekly-ebike-rental-rates/) before you book.

Before you set off, check the battery is full and ask what range the shop expects for the distance
you plan to ride. Our [pre-rental checklist](/blog/ebike-rental-checklist/) covers the rest. If
something goes wrong mid-ride, there are free self-service repair stands at the Venice Train Depot,
Ashton, Sarasota Springs and Pompano trailheads.

## FAQs

### How long is the Legacy Trail?

18.5 miles from the Historic Venice Train Depot to Fruitville Road in Sarasota. With the Venetian
Waterway Park paths at the Venice end, the connected paved network is about 20 miles.

### Are e-bikes allowed on the Legacy Trail?

Yes. Sarasota County permits e-bikes with functional pedals. The speed limit is 15 mph for everyone,
and other motorised vehicles are not allowed.

### Where is the best place to start the Legacy Trail?

The Venice Train Depot for the most scenic ride, heading north past Nokomis and Oscar Scherer State
Park. Osprey Junction and Culverhouse Nature Park are good mid-trail starts, and Payne Park is the
choice if you are staying in downtown Sarasota.

### How long does it take to ride the Legacy Trail on an e-bike?

At the 15 mph limit, the full 18.5 miles takes a little over an hour of riding, more like 90 minutes
to two hours with stops. An out-and-back of the whole trail is a half-day ride.

### Is there parking on the Legacy Trail?

Yes, at most trailheads, including the Venice Train Depot, Osprey Junction, Culverhouse Nature Park,
Sarasota Springs, Pompano and Payne Park.

### Is the Legacy Trail good for families?

Yes. It is flat and paved, and the busiest road crossings in the north are bridged. The Venice to
Osprey section has the most parks and restroom stops, which makes it the easiest family ride.

## The short version

Rent a Class 1 or 2 in Sarasota or Venice, start early at the Venice Train Depot, ride north past
Oscar Scherer to Osprey Junction and back, and keep to the 15 mph limit. Save the northern extension
for a second day, when you want to finish with lunch in downtown Sarasota.

Confirm opening hours with the shop before you drive over, especially out of season.
