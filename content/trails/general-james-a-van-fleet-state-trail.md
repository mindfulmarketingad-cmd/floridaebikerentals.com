The General James A. Van Fleet State Trail is 29.2 miles of paved path running dead straight through
the Green Swamp, from Polk City in the south to Mabel in the north. Its middle section is famously
about eighteen miles without a single road crossing.

That is the whole point of it. There is no town on this trail, almost no traffic, and very little
shade - just a ruler-straight corridor of asphalt through wetland, pine flatwoods and pasture, with
more wildlife than people on most weekdays.

## The route

The trail follows a former Seaboard Air Line rail corridor north-south across the Green Swamp
Wildlife Management Area, in three broad sections between its trailheads at Polk City, Green Pond
Road, Bay Lake Road and Mabel.

It forms part of the statewide [Coast to Coast Trail](/trails/florida-coast-to-coast-trail/), and it
is the section that gives that route its long, remote middle.

## The sections

**Polk City to Green Pond Road (about 9 miles).** Open, mostly pasture and pine, with a few crossings
at the southern end. The easiest access if you are coming from Lakeland or I-4.

**Green Pond Road to Bay Lake Road (about 10 miles).** The heart of the swamp. No crossings, no
buildings, cypress and wetland either side, and the best chance of seeing deer, otters, sandhill
cranes and alligators.

**Bay Lake Road to Mabel (about 10 miles).** Drier and more open again, running out toward the
northern trailhead in Lake County.

## Trailheads and parking

Four trailheads serve the trail: **Polk City** at the southern end, **Green Pond Road**, **Bay Lake
Road**, and **Mabel** at the north. All have free parking; Polk City and Mabel have restrooms.

For a first ride, park at Green Pond Road and ride north into the swamp. It puts the best miles
immediately in front of you and gives an easy turnaround.

## Conditions: read this before you go

This is the least forgiving paved trail in Central Florida, not because it is hard but because it is
empty.

- **There is no water for long stretches.** Fill up at the trailhead and carry more than you think.
- **Shade is minimal.** The corridor is open for most of its length.
- **Phone signal is patchy** across the middle section.
- **There is no food, no shop and no shelter** between trailheads.
- **Afternoon thunderstorms** in summer arrive fast, and there is nowhere to shelter.

Ride early, tell someone your plan, and carry a repair kit. A mechanical at mile 14 of the middle
section is a long walk.

## Wildlife

The Green Swamp is real wildlife country. Expect deer, wild turkey, sandhill cranes, hawks, snakes
sunning on the warm asphalt, and alligators in the ditches beside the trail. Give snakes room rather
than riding over them, and do not approach the water's edge.

## Why an e-bike suits this trail

Better than almost any trail in the state. Twenty-nine miles one way is a 58-mile round trip with no
shade, no water and no bail-out point - a serious day on a pedal bike, and a straightforward one with
assist. Pedal assist also means you can outrun a building thunderstorm to a trailhead, which is a
real safety margin here rather than a convenience.

Bring a fully charged battery and treat the manufacturer's range claim as optimistic. There is
nowhere to charge on this trail.

Class 1 and Class 2 bikes are the standard rentals and the classes commonly accepted on Florida
state trails; Class 3 rules vary by managing authority. See our
[e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/) and the
[Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/).

## FAQs

### How long is the Van Fleet State Trail?

29.2 miles, from Polk City in Polk County north to Mabel in Lake County, entirely paved.

### Is the Van Fleet Trail really 18 miles without a road crossing?

The middle of the trail, roughly between Green Pond Road and Bay Lake Road, has one of the longest
uninterrupted stretches of any paved trail in Florida - commonly cited as around eighteen miles with
no road crossings.

### Where should I park for the Van Fleet State Trail?

Polk City, Green Pond Road, Bay Lake Road and Mabel all have free trailhead parking. Green Pond Road
is the best starting point for a shorter ride into the best scenery.

### Is there shade or water on the Van Fleet Trail?

Very little shade, and water only at the trailheads. This is the trail on which to carry more than
you think you need, especially between May and September.

### Can you ride an e-bike on the Van Fleet State Trail?

E-bikes are commonly ridden on Florida's state trails, with Class 1 and Class 2 the usual rentals.
Confirm the current position for Class 3 with the Florida Department of Environmental Protection or
your rental shop.

### Is the Van Fleet Trail good for families?

It is flat, paved and traffic-free, which is ideal - but it is also remote, exposed and long between
facilities. Ride a short out-and-back from a trailhead with children rather than committing to a
point-to-point.

### Where can I rent an e-bike near the Van Fleet Trail?

There is no shop on the trail. Lakeland, Winter Haven, Clermont and Groveland are the nearest
options, and Orlando shops will often deliver.

## The short version

Park at Green Pond Road, ride north into the Green Swamp, and turn around when your battery says so.
It is the emptiest, straightest, quietest paved trail in Central Florida - and the one where a fully
charged e-bike is a genuine advantage rather than a luxury.
