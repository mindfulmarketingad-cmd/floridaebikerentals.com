The Nature Coast State Trail is a Y-shaped, roughly 32-mile paved trail across Dixie, Levy and
Gilchrist counties, linking Cross City, Fanning Springs, Chiefland and Trenton on old Florida
railroad corridors.

Its centrepiece is the former railroad trestle across the Suwannee River at Fanning Springs - a long
steel bridge, converted for walking and cycling, high above one of the most famous rivers in the
South. It is one of the two or three best bridge crossings on any Florida trail.

## The shape of it

Unusually, this trail is not a line. The three arms meet near Fanning Springs:

- **The Cross City arm**, running west, the quietest of the three.
- **The Chiefland arm**, running south-east, the easiest for supplies and parking.
- **The Trenton arm**, running north-east across the Suwannee trestle - the one to ride.

That means you can base yourself at the junction and pick a direction rather than committing to a
point-to-point.

## The Suwannee River trestle

The old rail bridge at Fanning Springs is the reason most people drive here. It is long, high and
open, with the river below and cypress on both banks. Ride out onto it, stop in the middle, and take
the time.

Fanning Springs State Park sits alongside, and the spring itself is a legitimate mid-ride swim in
summer - a constant 72°F regardless of what the air is doing.

## Sections worth riding

**Fanning Springs to Trenton (about 9 miles).** The trestle, the river, and the run into Trenton's
small restored depot. If you ride one arm, ride this one.

**Fanning Springs to Chiefland (about 9 miles).** Easy, straight and useful - Chiefland has shops,
food and reliable parking.

**Fanning Springs to Cross City (about 14 miles).** The longest and emptiest arm, running west into
Dixie County pine country. Quiet to the point of solitude on a weekday.

## Trailheads and parking

Free parking at the trailheads in **Fanning Springs**, **Chiefland**, **Trenton** and **Cross City**.
Fanning Springs is the natural base - it has the park, the springs, restrooms, and the trestle a few
hundred yards away.

## Surface, shade and conditions

- **Paved** for the full length, generally well maintained.
- **Patchy shade**, better on the Trenton and Cross City arms.
- **Rural crossings** with good sight lines.
- **Wildlife** including deer, turkeys, gopher tortoises at the trail edge, and snakes on the warm
  asphalt.
- **Very light traffic** on the trail itself outside weekends.

North Florida is humid. From May to September, ride in the morning and use the springs afterwards.

## Why an e-bike suits this trail

The Y shape is the reason. Riding two arms in a day - Trenton out and back, then Chiefland - is
around 36 miles, which is a long day pedalling and a comfortable one with assist. Assist also makes
the Cross City arm, which is otherwise a 28-mile round trip through empty country with no services,
a reasonable proposition.

Class 1 and Class 2 are standard rentals and generally accepted on Florida's state trails; Class 3
rules vary by managing authority. See our [e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/) and
the [Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/).

## Practical notes

- **Water** at the trailheads and in the towns, not between them.
- **Fanning Springs State Park** charges an entry fee for the springs; the trail itself is free.
- **Bring a towel** if you plan to swim.
- **Rentals** are not on the trail. Gainesville is the nearest real cluster, with Crystal River and
  Dunnellon closer to the south. Book ahead or arrange delivery.

## FAQs

### How long is the Nature Coast State Trail?

About 32 miles in total across three arms, linking Cross City, Fanning Springs, Chiefland and
Trenton.

### What is the bridge on the Nature Coast State Trail?

A converted railroad trestle over the Suwannee River at Fanning Springs. It is the highlight of the
trail and one of the best bridge crossings on any Florida trail.

### Where should I start on the Nature Coast State Trail?

Fanning Springs. It sits at the junction of the three arms, has parking, restrooms and a state park,
and puts the Suwannee trestle within a few minutes of the car park.

### Can you swim at Fanning Springs?

Yes. Fanning Springs State Park has a spring run that holds about 72°F year-round. There is a park
entry fee separate from the trail.

### Can you ride an e-bike on the Nature Coast State Trail?

Yes. Class 1 and Class 2 are the usual rentals and are generally accepted on Florida state trails.
Confirm the current position on Class 3 with Florida State Parks.

### Where can I rent an e-bike near the Nature Coast State Trail?

There is no shop on the trail. Gainesville is the closest cluster, roughly an hour away, with Crystal
River and Dunnellon to the south. Several shops in the region deliver.

## The short version

Park at Fanning Springs, ride north-east across the Suwannee trestle to Trenton, come back, and swim.
It is the best half-day on a bike in north Florida, and the trestle alone justifies the drive.
