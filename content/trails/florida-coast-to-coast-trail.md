The Florida Coast to Coast Trail is the state's headline project: a roughly 250-mile paved corridor
running from the Gulf at St. Petersburg to the Atlantic at Titusville, crossing the peninsula
through Tampa, Lakeland, Clermont, Winter Garden, Orlando, Seminole County and Brevard.

It is not a single new path. It is a spine that stitches together trails that already existed - the
Pinellas Trail, the Withlacoochee and Van Fleet corridors, the West Orange Trail, the Cross Seminole
Trail, the Seminole Wekiva Trail and the Brevard end - into one signed cross-state route.

## What the route actually is

Think of it as a chain of named trails rather than one continuous ribbon of asphalt with a single
name on the signs. Riding west to east you pass through, roughly in order:

- **Pinellas County**, on the Fred Marquis Pinellas Trail and the Duke Energy Trail.
- **Hillsborough and Polk**, through Tampa, Plant City and Lakeland.
- **The Green Swamp**, on the General James A. Van Fleet State Trail and its connectors.
- **Lake County**, through Clermont and Groveland on the South Lake Trail.
- **West Orange**, on the West Orange Trail through Oakland and Winter Garden.
- **Orlando and Seminole**, via the Orlando urban trails, the Cross Seminole Trail and the Seminole
  Wekiva Trail.
- **Brevard**, on the eastern segments toward Titusville and the Atlantic.

The corridor has been closing its remaining gaps for years, and the last of them have been the
hardest. Some short connectors still run on-road or on shared shoulders. Check the current status
with the Florida Department of Environmental Protection's Office of Greenways and Trails before you
plan a through-ride rather than a day out.

## Riding it as day sections

Almost nobody rides 250 miles. The value of the Coast to Coast for a visitor is that it puts a
well-signed, mostly traffic-free paved trail within reach of nearly every Central Florida base.

**Winter Garden and the West Orange Trail (about 22 miles).** The best single day on the whole
corridor if you want towns, food and shade. Winter Garden's downtown sits directly on the trail.

**Clermont and the South Lake Trail (about 12 miles).** Rolling by Florida standards, with lake
views and the Clermont waterfront at one end.

**The Van Fleet State Trail (29 miles).** Dead straight through the Green Swamp, almost no road
crossings, and the most remote-feeling paved miles in Central Florida.

**Cross Seminole and Seminole Wekiva (about 45 miles combined).** Suburban and shaded, with Spring
Hammock Preserve as the highlight. See our [Cross Seminole Trail guide](/trails/cross-seminole-trail/).

**The Pinellas Trail (about 45 miles).** The Gulf end, and a full trip in its own right - see the
[Pinellas Trail guide](/trails/pinellas-trail/).

## Why an e-bike changes this route

The Coast to Coast is where pedal assist stops being a novelty and starts being the reason the ride
is possible. A 25-mile section is a comfortable morning on an e-bike and a committing day on a
pedal bike in Florida heat. Riders attempting the full crossing typically plan five to seven days on
pedal bikes; on e-bikes it is commonly done in three to four, with the battery, not the legs, as the
limiting factor.

That means charging matters more than fitness. Plan overnights where you can charge, and treat any
claimed range figure as optimistic once you add heat, headwind and a loaded rack.

## Surface, shade and conditions

The corridor is paved for nearly all of its length and generally well surfaced, but conditions vary
enormously between counties:

- **Shade** is good on the West Orange, Cross Seminole and parts of the Pinellas Trail, and close to
  non-existent on the Van Fleet and the Brevard sections.
- **Water** is reliable in the urban sections and genuinely scarce across the Green Swamp.
- **Road crossings** cluster in Orlando, Tampa and Pinellas, and thin out to almost nothing in the
  middle.
- **Wind** comes off whichever coast you are nearest, which usually means a headwind for the last
  hour of an out-and-back.

Inland Central Florida runs hotter than either coast because there is no sea breeze. From May to
September, be on the trail before 9am or wait for late afternoon.

## Where to start if you have one day

If you are staying in the Orlando resort corridor, drive to Winter Garden and ride the West Orange
Trail. If you are on the Gulf, ride the Pinellas Trail out of Dunedin. If you want the quiet,
long-distance feel of the corridor without a support plan, park at Polk City and ride north on the
Van Fleet.

## Practical notes

- **Signage** along the corridor is improving but inconsistent between counties. Carry a phone with
  an offline map rather than relying on trail signs alone.
- **Class 1 and Class 2** e-bikes are what local shops rent and what shared-use paths in these
  counties commonly allow. Class 3 rules are set locally and are the ones most often restricted -
  our [e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/) explains the difference and the
  [Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/) covers the state position.
- **Rentals** cluster at the ends and in the Orlando area. Renting in the middle of the state is
  harder; plan around St. Petersburg, Tampa, Winter Garden, Orlando or the Space Coast.
- **One-way rides** need a shuttle or a rideshare plan. Most shops will not collect a bike 60 miles
  from where you picked it up.

## FAQs

### How long is the Florida Coast to Coast Trail?

About 250 miles from St. Petersburg on the Gulf to Titusville on the Atlantic, linking existing
trails across Pinellas, Hillsborough, Polk, Lake, Orange, Seminole and Brevard counties.

### Is the Coast to Coast Trail finished?

Most of it is paved and rideable, and the corridor has been closing its last gaps in stages. A few
short connectors still use roads or shoulders. Check the Office of Greenways and Trails for the
current status before planning a continuous crossing.

### How many days does it take to ride Coast to Coast?

Five to seven days is typical on a pedal bike. On e-bikes, three to four days is common, with
overnight charging rather than fitness as the constraint.

### What is the best section of the Coast to Coast Trail for a day ride?

The West Orange Trail through Winter Garden, for shade, surface and a town centre on the trail. The
Van Fleet State Trail is the best choice if you want quiet and distance instead.

### Can you ride an e-bike on the Coast to Coast Trail?

Yes, and it is a popular way to cover it. Rules are set by each local authority along the route
rather than by the state, and Class 1 and Class 2 bikes - which is what rental shops supply - are
accepted on the shared-use paths that make up the corridor.

### Where can I rent an e-bike along the Coast to Coast Trail?

The ends and the Orlando area are easiest: St. Petersburg, Dunedin and Clearwater on the Gulf,
Winter Garden and Orlando in the middle, and the Cocoa and Titusville area at the Atlantic end.

## The short version

Do not think of the Coast to Coast as one ride unless you are set on the crossing. Think of it as
the reason there is a good paved trail within half an hour of almost anywhere in Central Florida.
Pick the section nearest your base, rent a Class 1 or 2, ride early in summer, and carry more water
than the distance suggests.
