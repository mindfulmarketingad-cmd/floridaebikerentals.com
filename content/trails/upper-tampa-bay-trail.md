The Upper Tampa Bay Trail is Hillsborough County's answer to the Pinellas Trail: a paved rail trail
running roughly eight miles north-west from Citrus Park toward Oldsmar and the Pinellas county line,
with a spur into Peterson Road Park.

It is shorter than the big-name Florida trails and better than its length suggests. The northern
half runs through wetland and cypress on long boardwalk sections, which is not what most people
expect from a suburban Tampa bike path.

## The route

The southern trailhead is at Ehrlich Road in Citrus Park. From there the trail runs north-west,
crossing Gunn Highway and Linebaugh Avenue, past the Peterson Road Park spur, and on through Channel
Park toward the county line near Oldsmar.

Because it follows an old rail corridor, it is flat and consistently graded for its whole length.
Road crossings are concentrated in the southern half; the northern half is almost entirely free of
them.

## The sections worth riding

**Ehrlich Road to Gunn Highway (about 2 miles).** Suburban, with the most crossings on the trail.
Fine, but not the reason to come.

**Gunn Highway to Peterson Road Park (about 3 miles).** Opens out into parkland. Restrooms, water
and parking at Peterson Road Park, which is the practical mid-trail base.

**Peterson Road Park to Channel Park and the north end (about 3 miles).** The best part. Boardwalk
through wetland, cypress on both sides, wading birds, and a genuine feeling of being somewhere other
than a Tampa suburb.

If you have one hour, park at Peterson Road Park and ride north.

## Trailheads and parking

The three practical parking options are the Ehrlich Road trailhead at the southern end, Peterson
Road Park in the middle, and the Channel Park area toward the north. Parking is free and rarely a
problem outside weekend mornings.

Peterson Road Park is the one to aim for: it has restrooms, water, shade and the best half of the
trail either side of it.

## Surface, shade and conditions

The asphalt is in good condition for most of the length. Expect:

- **Boardwalk** through the wetland sections, which is slick after rain and narrower than the
  asphalt. Slow down and take the turns wide.
- **Real shade** in the northern half - unusual and welcome in Hillsborough County in summer.
- **Exposed suburban stretches** at the southern end.
- **Standing water** on the low sections after heavy summer rain.

Mosquitoes are a genuine consideration in the wetland sections at dawn and dusk from June to
October. Ride mid-morning if you can.

## Why an e-bike suits this trail

Eight miles one way is a sixteen-mile round trip, which is comfortable on any bike. What pedal assist
buys you here is the option to add the Peterson Road spur, to ride the whole thing and still have the
legs for the Pinellas Trail on the same trip, and to cross the exposed southern section in July
without arriving at the boardwalk already cooked.

Class 1 and Class 2 bikes are what Tampa-area shops rent and what Hillsborough County's shared-use
paths commonly allow. Faster Class 3 bikes are the ones most often restricted on paths like this -
our [e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/) explains the difference and the
[Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/) covers the state rules.

## Practical notes

- **Water** is at the park trailheads, not between them.
- **Alligators** live in the wetland the boardwalks cross. Stay on the deck and do not stop to feed
  anything.
- **The county line** is the practical northern limit. The connection onward into Pinellas is a road
  ride, not a trail ride.
- **Lock the bikes** at trailheads; the lots are quiet on weekdays.

## FAQs

### How long is the Upper Tampa Bay Trail?

About eight miles one way, from the Ehrlich Road trailhead in Citrus Park north-west toward Oldsmar,
plus the Peterson Road Park spur. A full out-and-back is roughly sixteen miles.

### Where does the Upper Tampa Bay Trail start?

The southern trailhead is at Ehrlich Road in Citrus Park, Tampa. The northern end is near the
Hillsborough-Pinellas county line by Oldsmar.

### What is the best part of the Upper Tampa Bay Trail?

The northern section through Channel Park, where the trail runs on boardwalk through cypress wetland.
Park at Peterson Road Park and ride north for the shortest route to the good part.

### Can you ride an e-bike on the Upper Tampa Bay Trail?

E-bikes are commonly ridden here. As on all Florida shared-use paths the rules are set locally, and
Class 1 and Class 2 - what rental shops supply - are the accepted classes. Check on a Class 3.

### Is the Upper Tampa Bay Trail good for families?

Yes. It is flat, paved, short enough for young riders, and the mid-trail park has restrooms and
water. Take the boardwalk sections slowly with children.

### Does the Upper Tampa Bay Trail connect to the Pinellas Trail?

Not directly as a trail. The two systems come close at the county line near Oldsmar, but the
connection between them is on-road.

## The short version

Park at Peterson Road Park, ride north through the boardwalks, turn around when you have had enough.
It is the best two hours of trail riding inside Tampa's suburbs, and it pairs well with a day on the
Pinellas Trail across the bay.
