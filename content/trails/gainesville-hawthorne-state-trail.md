The Gainesville-Hawthorne State Trail is North Central Florida's marquee paved ride: about 16 miles
of asphalt from Gainesville's Boulware Springs Park east through Paynes Prairie Preserve State Park
and Lochloosa Wildlife Management Area to the small town of Hawthorne. Florida State Parks calls it a
historic rail bed turned greenway, with overlooks, prairie vistas and a grassy equestrian trail beside
the pavement.

It is shorter than the [Withlacoochee](/trails/withlacoochee-state-trail/) or
[Van Fleet](/trails/van-fleet-state-trail/) State Trails, but the Paynes Prairie stretch is why people
come. Wild horses, bison, alligators and birdlife use the prairie basin, and short paved spurs lead to
overlooks over the open land. The western half also has mild hills and curves that are unusual on a
Florida rail trail.

An e-bike suits the full out-and-back (about 32 miles) without turning it into a grind, and Class 1
or Class 2 assist is plenty on the pavement. November to April is the comfortable season; summer heat
and afternoon storms build quickly over the open prairie.

## Route at a glance

Mileage runs west to east from Gainesville. Florida State Parks' trail map marks the main stops:

- **Boulware Springs Trailhead (mile 0).** Boulware Springs City Park on SE 15th Street, Gainesville.
  The usual start, with parking at the old water-works building. The city manages the park; Florida
  State Parks lists this trailhead's seasonal hours separately from the Paynes Prairie portion (see
  Rules below). A Depot Trail connector continues toward downtown Gainesville for riders who want
  extra paved miles before or after the state trail.
- **Sweetwater Overlook and La Chua Trail (around miles 0.7 to 1.0).** Short spur to a prairie
  overlook, and the junction with the La Chua hiking trail into Paynes Prairie. La Chua is for
  walkers, not bikes; leave the e-bike secured and walk if you want the boardwalk and wildlife
  platform.
- **Alachua Lake Overlook spur (around mile 1.8).** Another short paved spur to a prairie view.
- **Red Wolf Pond and Prairie Creek Boardwalk (around miles 4.0 to 4.8).** Mid-prairie stops along
  the corridor.
- **Rochelle / Prairie Creek Preserve (around mile 6.4).** Parking at the intersection of county
  roads 234 and 2082. A useful mid-trail base if you want a shorter out-and-back from either end.
- **Phifer Flatwoods Preserve (around miles 9.3 to 9.9).** Marsh and swamp side trails off the paved
  corridor.
- **Lochloosa equestrian trailhead (around mile 13.8).** 7902 SE 200th Drive, off CR 2082 west of
  Hawthorne. Parking; the equestrian trail continues alongside the pavement.
- **Hawthorne Trailhead (around mile 15.2 to 16).** 2182 SE 71st Avenue, Hawthorne. Parking at the
  east end of the state trail; downtown Hawthorne is a short ride farther into town.

Florida State Parks lists four parking trailheads: Boulware Springs, Rochelle, the Lochloosa
equestrian lot and Hawthorne. The paved trail has a grassy equestrian trail running alongside for
much of the route; that shoulder is for horses, not bikes.

## The sections worth riding

**Boulware Springs to Rochelle and back (about 13 miles).** The pick for a first ride. You get the
shaded hammock, the prairie overlooks and the mild hills west of Rochelle without committing to the
full length. Start early if you want quieter wildlife viewing from the overlook spurs.

**Boulware Springs through Paynes Prairie to CR 325.** Roughly the western half of the trail, often
named as the most scenic stretch: canopy, prairie edges and the short hills near the Alachua Lake
Overlook spur. Turn around when you have used about half your battery and water.

**Rochelle to Hawthorne and back.** Flatter and more open, with less shade and a quieter rural feel
as you approach Lochloosa and Hawthorne. Good if you are staging from the east end.

**The whole thing, one way or out-and-back.** Sixteen miles one way is an easy e-bike morning on
moderate assist. An out-and-back of about 32 miles fits most rental batteries if you start full and
keep the assist modest. Ask the shop what range to expect before you plan the round trip, and carry
more water than the distance suggests — services thin out once you leave Gainesville.

## Rules on the trail

- **E-bikes are allowed.** Florida State Parks says bicycles, including electric bicycles, may use
  trails designated for bicycle use, consistent with Section 316.20655 of Florida law. The park's
  [experiences page](https://www.floridastateparks.org/parks-and-trails/gainesville-hawthorne-state-trail/experiences-amenities)
  is the current word.
- **Obey posted speed limits** and ride at a safe speed. Drop the assist near walkers and horses; our
  [e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/) explains what each class can do.
- **Cyclists yield to other users, and everyone yields to horses.** Keep right, pass on the left and
  give a clear warning when you pass. Cross traffic at road crossings does not stop — you do.
- **Hours.** Florida State Parks lists the trail at 8 a.m. to sunset, 365 days a year, with **no
  fee**. The Boulware Springs trailhead has its own seasonal hours on the park's hours page (8 a.m.
  to 6 p.m. November through April; 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. May through October), and the Paynes Prairie
  portion is open 8 a.m. until sundown year-round. The city-managed Boulware Springs parking area is
  listed separately on the park's main page; confirm current lot hours before you leave a car there
  late in the day.
- **Dogs are welcome** on a leash no longer than six feet on the Gainesville-Hawthorne State Trail.
  Separate Paynes Prairie hiking trails (including La Chua) have their own pet rules — check before
  you walk in with a dog.
- **Helmets** are recommended for everyone. Florida law requires them for riders under 16.

Our [Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/) covers the rest of the state rules.

## Renting for the Gainesville-Hawthorne State Trail

There is no rental desk at the trailheads. You rent in Gainesville and ride or drive to Boulware
Springs, so confirm with the shop that the bike can leave their local area and that you have enough
range for your plan.

In our directory, **E-Bike Superstore authorized Pedego Dealer** on West University Avenue lists
electric-bike sales and rentals. Ratings, hours and directions are on the
[E-Bike Superstore listing](/cities/gainesville/e-bike-superstore-authorized-pedego-dealer/).
**Trek Bicycle Gainesville** on NW 13th Street is also listed as an electric bicycle store (sales and
service; not marked as a rental shop in our data). Compare both on our
[Gainesville](/cities/gainesville/) page, where the nearest shops also appear at the foot of this
guide.

A comfortable Class 1 or Class 2 cruiser or
[commuter e-bike](/electric-bike-types/commuter-electric-bikes/) is the right bike for this pavement.
For a multi-day stay, compare [daily and weekly rates](/costs/daily-vs-weekly-ebike-rental-rates/)
and run through our [pre-rental checklist](/blog/ebike-rental-checklist/) before you leave the lot.

## FAQs

### How long is the Gainesville-Hawthorne State Trail?

About 16 miles one way from Boulware Springs in Gainesville to Hawthorne, through Paynes Prairie
Preserve State Park and other conservation lands.

### Are e-bikes allowed on the Gainesville-Hawthorne State Trail?

Yes. Florida State Parks allows electric bicycles on trails designated for bicycle use, consistent
with Section 316.20655 of Florida law. Ride at a safe speed and yield to walkers and horses.

### Where is the best place to start the Gainesville-Hawthorne State Trail?

Boulware Springs for the full prairie experience and the overlook spurs. Rochelle if you want a
mid-trail start with parking. Hawthorne if you are coming from the east and prefer the quieter rural
half.

### What are the trail's hours, and is there a fee?

Florida State Parks lists the trail 8 a.m. to sunset every day with no fee. Boulware Springs and the
Paynes Prairie portion have the seasonal details on the park's hours page — check those if you are
parking at the Gainesville end late in the day.

### Can you see wildlife from the trail?

Paynes Prairie is known for wild horses, bison, alligators and birdlife. Overlook spurs along the
western half are the usual viewing stops from the bike; the La Chua Trail is a separate hiking route
into the prairie if you want to walk. Give wildlife space and never feed animals.

### Can you ride the whole trail in a day on an e-bike?

One way, easily. An out-and-back of about 32 miles is a comfortable day on most rental e-bikes if
you start with a full charge. Confirm range with the shop before you commit.

## The short version

Rent in Gainesville, start after 8 a.m. at Boulware Springs, ride east through the Paynes Prairie
overlooks at least as far as Rochelle, and carry more water than the distance suggests. It is the
best paved wildlife ride in North Central Florida, and late autumn through spring is the time to do
it.

Confirm opening hours with the shop before you drive over.
