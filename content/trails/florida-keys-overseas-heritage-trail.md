The Florida Keys Overseas Heritage Trail is the ride people picture when they think of e-biking the
Keys: a path beside US 1 that island-hops from Key Largo to Key West over the bones of Henry Flagler's
railroad. Florida State Parks describes it as more than 90 linear miles of existing trail, paved in
segments along a planned 106-mile corridor, and it uses 23 of the historic Flagler railroad bridges.

It is not one continuous greenway, and that is the most useful thing to know before you rent. Some
stretches are separated path with water on both sides; others are a sidewalk-style path past shops
and driveways; a few gaps put you on the highway shoulder. Pick the right section and it is one of
the best rides in Florida. Try to ride the whole thing on a rental and you will spend a lot of it next
to traffic on US 1.

An e-bike earns its keep here because of the **wind**, not hills. The Keys are flat, but a steady
breeze across open water can make a 10-mile return leg feel twice as long. Check the forecast, ride
into the wind first, and let the assist carry you home.

November to April is the season. Summer brings heat with almost no shade on the bridges and
afternoon storms that build quickly over the water.

## Route at a glance

Distances below are approximate and follow the trail's own sections, south to north. Mile markers
(MM) are the green US 1 markers that the whole Keys uses for addresses.

- **Lower Keys: Key West to Bahia Honda (about 38 miles).** Starts in Key West, where Bayview Park on Truman Avenue is a listed trailhead, and runs past Smathers Beach and up through Big Pine Key, home of the Key Deer National Wildlife Refuge. Historic railroad bridges cross mangrove-lined channels.
- **Middle Keys: Bahia Honda to Long Key and Channel Five (about 43 miles).** The most island-hopping and the most tropical-looking section: Marathon, the Seven Mile Bridge, Curry Hammock and Long Key State Parks, the Spandrel Arch Bridge at Tom's Harbor and the fishing platforms of the historic Long Key Bridge.
- **Upper Keys: Islamorada to Key Largo (about 27 miles).** The busiest, most built-up section, with fewer water views and more tropical hammock between homes and businesses. Florida State Parks says the **longest continuous paved stretch** is here, roughly from MM 106 in Key Largo down through MM 72 in Islamorada. The northern end is Dagny Johnson Key Largo Hammock Botanical State Park, at County Road 905 and MM 106.

Some old fishing bridges along the corridor are closed for safety: Florida State Parks lists
Ohio-Missouri, Ohio-Bahia Honda, Missouri-Little Duck, Lower Sugarloaf and Niles. Follow the posted
detours on the day.

## The sections worth riding

**The Old Seven Mile Bridge to Pigeon Key (Marathon, about 4.4 miles round trip).** The headline
ride. The restored 2.2-mile span of the old railroad bridge reopened to walkers, runners and cyclists
in January 2022, and it is car-free. It starts at the west end of Marathon near MM 47, by Knights Key.
The Pigeon Key Foundation says the bridge is open to bikers, but **admission is required to get onto
Pigeon Key itself**, use its restrooms or join a tour, and the island's posted hours are 9 a.m. to
4 p.m. The bridge deck has almost no shade, so go early. Parking at the base is limited; riding in on
the trail from a Marathon rental avoids the lot altogether. Florida State Parks notes picnic tables
(no restrooms) at the Knights Key trailhead at the north end of the bridge.

**Marathon to Curry Hammock State Park and Grassy Key.** Head east from Marathon toward Grassy Key for
a flat out-and-back with water views and a state park beach as the turnaround. Combine it with the
Old Seven Mile Bridge for a full Marathon day.

**Islamorada: Anne's Beach to Founders Park.** A good Upper Keys sampler on the long continuous
section. Anne's Beach and the Upper Matecumbe waysides are at the south end; Founders Park in
Islamorada makes an easy stop with shade and facilities.

**Key Largo: John Pennekamp to Key Largo Hammock.** The north end of the trail. Short, flat and
mostly through hammock rather than over water, which makes it the shadiest stretch on a hot day.

**Skip on a rental: the new Seven Mile Bridge.** There is no separate path across the modern bridge;
it is the Overseas Highway itself, and riders who cross share it with fast traffic. Florida State
Parks asks cyclists to use extreme caution along the bridges. Most visitors are better off riding the
old bridge to Pigeon Key and turning around.

## Rules on the trail

- **E-bikes are allowed.** Florida State Parks says bicycles, including electric bicycles, may be used on trails designated for bicycle use, consistent with [section 316.20655](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.20655) of Florida law. [Florida State Parks](https://www.floridastateparks.org/learn/explore-bike) does not split that permission by class. Some sources say only Class 1 is welcome; check the park's [experiences page](https://www.floridastateparks.org/parks-and-trails/florida-keys-overseas-heritage-trail/experiences-amenities) for current wording, and ride at a safe speed whatever your class. Our [e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/) explains the difference.
- **Old Seven Mile Bridge.** The [Pigeon Key Foundation](https://pigeonkey.net/old-7-mile-bridge-open-to-public/) says the bridge is open to walkers, bikers and runners, and that no motorized vehicles are allowed. It does not say whether an e-bike counts. Scooters and mopeds are motorized vehicles. If you are on an e-bike, read the sign at the gate before you ride on.
- **Ride with traffic and obey traffic laws** where the trail runs on road shoulders or crosses driveways and side streets along US 1. Cross traffic does not stop for you.
- **Hours and fees.** Florida State Parks lists the trail as open 8 a.m. to sunset, 365 days a year, with **no fee**. State parks along the route charge their own entry fees.
- **Helmets** are recommended for everyone. [Florida law](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.2065) requires them for riders and passengers under 16.
- **Pack water, sunscreen and bug spray,** which is the park's own advice. Shade and water are scarce between towns, especially in the Middle Keys.

Our [Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/) covers the rest of the state rules.

## Renting for the Overseas Heritage Trail

There is no rental desk on the trail itself. Rent in the town nearest the section you want and ride
from the shop where you can. Ask whether the shop delivers to your rental or hotel, whether its bikes
are allowed off the island, and how far a full charge goes into a headwind.

From our directory, by area:

- **Key Largo.** [All Keys Cycles](/cities/key-largo/all-keys-cycles/) and [Jack's Bike](/cities/key-largo/jacks-bike/) on the Overseas Highway are both listed as bike shops with rentals. Compare them on the [Key Largo](/cities/key-largo/) page.
- **Islamorada.** [Mai D'cor Rentals](/cities/islamorada/mai-dcor-rentals/) is listed for bicycle rentals alongside golf carts and kayaks.
- **Marathon, for the Seven Mile Bridge.** [Marathon Bikeworks](/cities/marathon/marathon-bikeworks/) is listed for bike and e-bike rentals, repairs and sales, and [Keys Vacation Supply Rentals](/cities/marathon/keys-vacation-supply-rentals/) as a bike and electric-bike shop. Both are on the [Marathon](/cities/marathon/) page.
- **Big Pine Key, for the Lower Keys.** [Big Pine Key Bicycle Center](/cities/big-pine-key/big-pine-key-bicycle-center/) lists rentals and repairs. See the [Big Pine Key](/cities/big-pine-key/) page.

## FAQs

### How long is the Florida Keys Overseas Heritage Trail?

Florida State Parks describes more than 90 miles of existing trail, paved in segments, along a planned
106-mile corridor from Key Largo to Key West. Trail guides split it into the Lower Keys (about 38
miles), Middle Keys (about 43 miles) and Upper Keys (about 27 miles).

### Are e-bikes allowed on the Overseas Heritage Trail?

Yes. Florida State Parks allows bicycles, including electric bicycles, on trails designated for
bicycle use, consistent with [section 316.20655](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.20655) of Florida law. Ride at a safe speed and yield to
walkers.

### Can you ride an e-bike on the Old Seven Mile Bridge?

Yes. The restored 2.2-mile span from Marathon to Pigeon Key is open to cyclists, walkers and runners
and closed to motorized vehicles. Getting onto Pigeon Key itself requires an admission ticket from the
Pigeon Key Foundation.

### Can you bike from Key Largo to Key West?

You can follow the corridor the whole way, but it is not continuous path. Gaps and bridges put you on
the US 1 shoulder, including the new Seven Mile Bridge. On a rental e-bike, most people ride one
section at a time rather than the full distance.

### What is the best section for a first ride?

The Old Seven Mile Bridge to Pigeon Key from Marathon. It is short, car-free and the most famous view
on the trail. For a longer ride on separated path, try the continuous stretch through Islamorada and
Key Largo.

### Does the trail have hours or a fee?

Florida State Parks lists the trail as open 8 a.m. to sunset every day with no fee. State parks you
pass, and Pigeon Key, charge their own admission.

## The short version

Rent in Marathon, ride the Old Seven Mile Bridge to Pigeon Key early in the morning, then head east
toward Curry Hammock if you want more. For longer separated-path miles, base yourself in Islamorada or
Key Largo. Ride into the wind first, carry more water than you think, and leave the new Seven Mile
Bridge to the cars.

Confirm opening hours and rental terms with the shop before you go.
