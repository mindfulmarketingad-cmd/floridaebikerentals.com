The Florida Keys Overseas Heritage Trail follows the route of Henry Flagler's Overseas Railroad down
the length of the Keys: 106 miles from Key Largo to Key West, of which roughly 90 miles are built as
paved trail, with the remainder still on the shoulder of US-1.

It is the most scenic paved trail in Florida and the least continuous. Managed as a Florida State
Park, it strings together sections of separated path, restored historic railway bridges, and
stretches where you are riding beside the only road in the Keys.

## What the trail is really like

Where the trail is separated, it is superb: open water on both sides, old bridge piers marching off
toward the horizon, and the kind of light that makes people stop every mile for photographs.

Where it is not separated, you are on the shoulder of US-1 with traffic passing at 55mph. Those
sections are legal and ridden daily, but they are not what most people picture when they hear
"rail trail", and they are the reason this route needs planning rather than improvisation.

## The historic bridges

The restored railway bridges are the highlights and are worth building a ride around:

- **Old Seven Mile Bridge**, running west from Marathon toward Pigeon Key. Reopened after a major
  restoration and now the single best stretch of the whole trail.
- **The Old Bahia Honda Bridge area**, best seen from Bahia Honda State Park.
- **Long Key, Channel Five and Indian Key** bridge sections through the middle Keys.

Some historic bridge segments remain closed for works at any given time. Check current bridge status
with Florida State Parks before you plan a ride around one.

## Sections worth riding

**Key Largo (MM 106-90).** The longest continuous separated section and the easiest for a first ride.

**Islamorada (MM 90-72).** Mixed separated path and roadside, with water views for most of it.

**Marathon and the Old Seven Mile Bridge (MM 53-40).** The section to ride if you ride only one. Park
in Marathon, ride out onto the old bridge, turn around at Pigeon Key.

**Big Pine Key and the Lower Keys (MM 33-9).** Quiet, with Key deer on and beside the trail. Ride
slowly and give them room.

**Key West (MM 9-0).** Town riding rather than trail riding, and the natural place to end. See our
[Key West e-bike guide](/blog/key-west-ebike-guide/) for riding in the town itself.

## Wind, sun and heat

There is no shade in the Keys. None that matters, anyway. Add unfiltered sun, humidity and a
persistent sea wind that is a pleasure in one direction and a wall in the other, and the Keys become
harder riding than the flat profile suggests.

Plan out-and-backs so the wind is behind you on the way home, not the way out. Carry more water than
you would inland. Reef-safe sunscreen and a hat under the helmet are not optional.

## Why an e-bike suits this trail

Better than almost anywhere in Florida. Distances between the good sections are long, the wind is the
real terrain, and every mile is exposed. Pedal assist is what turns a 20-mile Keys ride from an
endurance exercise into a sightseeing morning - and it is what gets you home into a headwind at the
end of it.

Class 1 and Class 2 bikes are what Keys shops rent. Local rules on shared paths are set by the
managing authority, and faster Class 3 bikes are the ones most often restricted - see our
[e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/) and the
[Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/).

## Safety on the roadside sections

Treat them as road riding, because they are:

- Ride with traffic, single file, as far right as is safe.
- Use lights front and rear, day and night. Visibility against glare off the water is poor.
- Wear something bright. The Keys have heavy tourist traffic and a lot of drivers looking at the
  view rather than the shoulder.
- Cross bridges with care where the shoulder narrows. If a bridge feels wrong, wait for a gap rather
  than committing.

## FAQs

### How long is the Florida Keys Overseas Heritage Trail?

106 miles from Key Largo to Key West, of which roughly 90 miles are built as paved trail. The
remainder is ridden on the shoulder of US-1.

### Is the Overseas Heritage Trail continuous?

No. It is a series of separated sections linked by on-road stretches along US-1. Florida State Parks
manages it and publishes the current status of each section and historic bridge.

### Can you ride across the Seven Mile Bridge?

The restored Old Seven Mile Bridge is open for walking and cycling from Marathon out toward Pigeon
Key. It is the highlight of the whole trail. Check current opening with Florida State Parks, as
sections close for maintenance.

### Can you ride an e-bike on the Overseas Heritage Trail?

Yes, and e-bikes are the most common rental in the Keys. Class 1 and Class 2 are standard; check the
current position on Class 3 with your rental shop.

### How many days does it take to ride the Keys end to end?

Three to four days on a pedal bike is typical. On an e-bike, two to three is realistic, with charging
and wind rather than fitness as the constraints.

### Where should I rent an e-bike in the Keys?

Key West has by far the most shops, with Key Largo, Islamorada, Marathon and Big Pine Key all served
as well. Rent where you are staying - one-way rentals down the Keys are rarely offered.

### Is the Overseas Heritage Trail good for families?

The separated sections are, particularly around Key Largo and Marathon. The on-road sections beside
US-1 are not suitable for children. Pick a separated section and ride it as an out-and-back.

## The short version

Do not plan to ride the Keys end to end unless that is the point of your trip. Pick a section -
Marathon and the Old Seven Mile Bridge first, then Key Largo - rent a Class 1 or 2 where you are
staying, ride into the wind first, and treat sun and water as the real logistics.
