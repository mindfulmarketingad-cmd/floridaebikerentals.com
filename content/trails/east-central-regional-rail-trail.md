The East Central Regional Rail Trail - the ECRRT - is the eastern arm of Florida's cross-state trail
network, a roughly 52-mile paved corridor being built across Volusia and Brevard counties on a former
Florida East Coast Railway line.

It runs, broadly, from the Edgewater and New Smyrna Beach area west and south through Osteen and
Maytown toward Titusville, where it meets the Atlantic end of the
[Coast to Coast Trail](/trails/florida-coast-to-coast-trail/).

## What is open

The trail has been built in segments over many years, and long stretches are paved and rideable while
a few connectors remain in progress. Rather than trusting any single published mileage, check the
current status with Volusia County or the Florida Department of Environmental Protection's Office of
Greenways and Trails before you drive to a trailhead.

The consistently good riding is on the Edgewater-to-Maytown corridor and the western segments around
Osteen and the Guise Road area.

## The character of the trail

This is not a town-to-town trail with cafés on it. The ECRRT runs through pine flatwoods, palmetto
scrub, pasture and marsh, with long straight stretches, few road crossings and very little between
trailheads.

The Maytown Road section is the emptiest paved riding on Florida's east side: dead straight, no
services, and often no other riders on a weekday. That is either the appeal or the reason to ride
somewhere else, depending on what you want from a morning.

## Sections worth riding

**Edgewater west (about 8 miles).** The most accessible end, closest to New Smyrna Beach and the
coast. Good surface, easy parking, and a sensible first ride.

**The Maytown stretch.** Long, straight and remote, running west across the flatwoods. Best ridden
early, and best ridden with a full battery.

**Osteen and the western segments.** Closer to Deltona and Sanford, and the natural approach if you
are staying in Central Florida rather than on the coast.

## Conditions

- **Shade is minimal.** The corridor is open for most of its length.
- **Water and restrooms** are at trailheads only, and the trailheads are far apart.
- **Wildlife** is genuinely abundant - hawks, deer, wild pigs, snakes on the warm asphalt, and
  alligators in the roadside ditches.
- **Phone signal** is unreliable through the middle.
- **Summer storms** build fast over the flatwoods with nowhere to shelter.

Ride before 10am from May to September, carry more water than the distance suggests, and tell
someone your plan.

## Why an e-bike suits this trail

The ECRRT is exactly the profile where assist matters: long, flat, exposed and empty. A 20-mile
out-and-back on the Maytown section is unremarkable on an e-bike and a grind on a pedal bike in
August heat. Assist also gives you the speed to get back to a trailhead ahead of an afternoon storm.

Charge fully before you set out. There is nowhere on the trail to plug in.

Class 1 and Class 2 are the standard rentals and what is generally accepted on Florida's paved public
trails; Class 3 rules vary by managing authority. See our
[e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/) and the
[Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/).

## Practical notes

- **Rentals** are on the coast, not on the trail: New Smyrna Beach, Daytona Beach and the Cocoa area
  are the nearest clusters, and shops there commonly deliver.
- **Parking** at the marked trailheads is free and rarely busy.
- **Repair kit** is not optional on the remote sections.
- **Sunscreen and a hat** matter more here than on any shaded Central Florida trail.

## FAQs

### How long is the East Central Regional Rail Trail?

About 52 miles when complete, running across Volusia and Brevard counties. Long segments are open and
paved; a few connectors remain in progress, so check current status before you go.

### Where does the East Central Regional Rail Trail go?

Broadly from the Edgewater and New Smyrna Beach area west and south through Osteen and Maytown toward
Titusville, where it joins the Atlantic end of the statewide Coast to Coast Trail.

### Is the East Central Regional Rail Trail finished?

Not entirely. It has been built in phases and some connectors are still being completed. Volusia
County and the state Office of Greenways and Trails publish the current status.

### Is there shade on the East Central Regional Rail Trail?

Very little. The corridor is open flatwoods and pasture for most of its length, which makes early
starts essential in summer.

### Can you ride an e-bike on the East Central Regional Rail Trail?

Yes, e-bikes are commonly ridden on it. Class 1 and Class 2 are the usual rental classes and the ones
generally accepted; confirm Class 3 with the county before riding.

### Where can I rent an e-bike near the East Central Regional Rail Trail?

New Smyrna Beach, Edgewater, Daytona Beach and the Cocoa area have the closest rental shops. Nothing
rents on the trail itself, so plan a short drive or delivery.

## The short version

Start at the Edgewater end for an easy first ride, or take the Maytown section if you want empty,
straight, quiet miles. Full battery, full bottles, early start - the ECRRT rewards preparation and
punishes turning up at noon in July.
