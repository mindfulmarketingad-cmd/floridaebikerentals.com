The Seminole Wekiva Trail runs roughly fourteen miles up the western side of Seminole County, from
the San Sebastian Prong trailhead in Altamonte Springs north through Longwood and Lake Mary to
Markham Road near the Wekiva River.

It is the quieter sibling of the Cross Seminole Trail, and the two together form the Seminole County
half of the cross-state Coast to Coast route. If you are staying anywhere north of Orlando, this is
the trail closest to you.

## The route

From Altamonte Springs the trail runs north on a former rail corridor, paralleling the SR 434
corridor and threading behind neighbourhoods, parks and light industrial land. It passes the Sabal
Point and Springs area, crosses into Longwood and Lake Mary, and finishes at Markham Road on the
edge of the Wekiva basin.

At its southern end it connects toward the Altamonte Springs trail network; at its northern end it
links across the Interstate 4 pedestrian bridge to the [Cross Seminole
Trail](/trails/cross-seminole-trail/), which is what makes a 14-mile trail into a 45-mile day if you
want one.

## Riding it in sections

**San Sebastian Prong to Jones Trailhead (about 4 miles).** Suburban and busy with local riders,
walkers and commuters. More road crossings than the rest.

**Jones to Sanlando and Wekiva Springs Road (about 5 miles).** The middle. Better shade, fewer
crossings, and the section that feels most like a trail rather than a path between car parks.

**North to Markham Road (about 5 miles).** Quietest, greenest, and the run-in to the Wekiva basin.
Turn around here or continue toward the I-4 bridge and the Cross Seminole.

## Trailheads and parking

Parking is free at the county trailheads along the route, including San Sebastian Prong in Altamonte
Springs, the Jones and Sanlando area trailheads, and the northern end near Markham Road. Weekend
mornings fill the southern lots first.

For a first ride, start in the middle and ride north: you skip the busiest crossings and get the
better scenery immediately.

## Surface, shade and conditions

Paved end to end and generally well maintained. Expect:

- **Road crossings** concentrated in the southern third. Most are marked or signalised.
- **Patchy shade** - genuinely good in places, absent in others. Not a trail to ride at midday in
  July.
- **A rolling profile** by Florida standards, with a few short rises around the Wekiva side.
- **Local traffic** on foot: this is a neighbourhood trail as much as a destination one, so expect
  dog walkers and runners near the trailheads.

Inland Seminole County gets no sea breeze. From May to September, ride before 10am.

## Why an e-bike suits this trail

Fourteen miles one way is a 28-mile round trip - a long afternoon on a pedal bike in Florida heat and
a relaxed morning with assist. More to the point, the northern connection to the Cross Seminole Trail
means the natural ride here is longer than the trail's own length, and that is exactly the situation
where a motor turns a planned two hours into an easy four.

Class 1 and Class 2 are what local shops rent and what Seminole County's shared-use paths commonly
allow. Class 3 rules are set locally and are the ones most often restricted - see our
[e-bike classes guide](/blog/ebike-classes-explained/) and the
[Florida e-bike law guide](/blog/florida-ebike-laws/).

## Practical notes

- **Trail contact:** Seminole County Leisure Services, 407-665-2001.
- **Water** is at the trailhead parks, not in between.
- **The I-4 pedestrian bridge** at the northern end is the link to the Cross Seminole Trail. Ride it
  even if you turn round straight after.
- **Rentals** are easiest in Orlando, Winter Garden and Sanford; there is no shop directly on this
  trail, so plan on delivery or a short drive.

## FAQs

### How long is the Seminole Wekiva Trail?

About fourteen miles, running from Altamonte Springs north to Markham Road in Seminole County. A full
out-and-back is roughly 28 miles.

### Where does the Seminole Wekiva Trail start and end?

The southern end is the San Sebastian Prong trailhead in Altamonte Springs. The northern end is at
Markham Road, with a connection across the I-4 pedestrian bridge to the Cross Seminole Trail.

### Does the Seminole Wekiva Trail connect to the Cross Seminole Trail?

Yes, at the northern end via the Interstate 4 pedestrian bridge near Lake Mary. Together they form
the Seminole County section of the statewide Coast to Coast Trail.

### Can you ride an e-bike on the Seminole Wekiva Trail?

E-bikes are commonly ridden here. Rules are set by Seminole County rather than the state; Class 1 and
Class 2 are the standard rental classes and the ones generally accepted on shared-use paths. Confirm
a Class 3 before you ride.

### Is the Seminole Wekiva Trail busy?

The southern few miles are, especially at weekends - it doubles as a neighbourhood commuter route.
The northern half is much quieter.

### Where can I rent an e-bike for the Seminole Wekiva Trail?

There is no shop on the trail itself. Orlando, Winter Garden and Sanford are the nearest rental
clusters, and several shops in the area deliver.

## The short version

Start mid-trail, ride north to Markham Road, and cross the I-4 bridge onto the Cross Seminole if you
still have battery. It is the most convenient real trail ride for anyone staying north of Orlando,
and it is quieter than everything else in the county.
