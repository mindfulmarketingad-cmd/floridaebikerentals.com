## The Riverwalk and Bayshore Boulevard

**The Tampa Riverwalk (2.6 miles).** A multi-use path along the Hillsborough River and the Garrison
Channel, from Armature Works and the Heights at the north end, past Curtis Hixon Park and the
museums, to Sparkman Wharf. It is the city's showpiece, which is also its problem: it is busy with
walkers, especially on weekend evenings. Treat it as a slow cruise, not a ride to cover miles.

**Bayshore Boulevard (4.5 miles).** A 10-foot-wide waterfront sidewalk from Platt Street to Gandy
Boulevard, along Hillsborough Bay, past the historic houses of Hyde Park. The City of Tampa calls it
one of the longest continuous sidewalks in the United States, and it is the classic Tampa ride.
There are also on-road bike lanes along much of the boulevard, which is where faster riders belong.
Out and back is about 9 miles; Hyde Park Village is the obvious stop.

**Riverwalk plus Bayshore.** The Platt Street Bridge links the south end of downtown to the start of
Bayshore, which makes a natural half-day: start at Armature Works, ride the Riverwalk south, cross
onto Bayshore, ride to Gandy and back. With a lunch stop it fills a two- to four-hour rental
comfortably.

**The Selmon Greenway (1.7 miles).** An east-west path through downtown, mostly in the shade of the
expressway, starting where Brorein Street meets the Riverwalk and connecting with the Meridian Avenue
Trail toward the Channel District and Ybor City. Useful on a hot afternoon.

**Davis Islands.** Just across the channel from downtown and quiet by Tampa standards: palm-lined
streets, the waterfront and the small airport. The City lists it among its popular places to bike,
and on a rental it makes a pleasant add-on to Bayshore.

## Tampa's local rules, and what is only proposed

These are the local rules on top of the statewide ones below, as of October 31, 2026. Rules are
moving quickly in Tampa Bay, so read the signs where you ride and ask your rental shop what applies
on the day.

- **In force: e-bikes are allowed on City of Tampa trails, at up to 15 mph.** The City's greenways and trails FAQ lists electric bicycles as allowed on city trails and says all city trails have a 15 mph speed limit; the city code adds "unless otherwise posted", so a sign can set a lower limit. Gas-powered bikes are not allowed.
- **In force: app bikes stay off the Riverwalk and Bayshore.** Under the City's shared micromobility program, rented Lime and Spin e-bikes and scooters may not be ridden on the Riverwalk, Bayshore Boulevard or 7th Avenue in Ybor. That rule is for the app fleets; a bike from a rental shop or your own bike follows the trail rule above.
- **Rejected: a 10 mph city trail limit.** In December 2025 Tampa City Council unanimously voted down an ordinance that would have set a 10 mph limit on city park trails and banned wheelies and riding more than two abreast. Council's wider bike and pedestrian safety discussion has been continued to January 7, 2027, so the issue may return.
- **Proposed, not law: Hillsborough County speed limits.** A county ordinance requested by the Sheriff's Office would cap e-bikes, scooters and ordinary bicycles at 10 mph on sidewalks, 15 mph on shared-use paths and trails, and 28 mph on roads, bike lanes and bike paths. As reported, it would also ban wheelies, stunts and street takeovers, require riders to yield to people walking and call out before passing, and let deputies cite riders and impound bikes. County commissioners set a public hearing for November 10, 2026. Checked that day, we had not seen an adopted ordinance. If it is adopted later, it would apply in Tampa wherever the city has not set its own rules. Until then, none of it is in force.

None of that changes what a sensible rider does anyway: ride slowly where people are walking, ring
or call out before passing, and use the road bike lanes on Bayshore if you want to go faster.

## FAQs

### Can you ride an e-bike on the Tampa Riverwalk?

Yes, on a shop rental or your own bike. The City of Tampa allows electric bicycles on its trails at
up to 15 mph unless a sign says less, but the Riverwalk is crowded with walkers, so ride it at
walking-plus pace. Lime and Spin app e-bikes are not allowed on the Riverwalk.

### Can you ride an e-bike on Bayshore Boulevard?

Yes. You can ride the wide waterfront sidewalk, shared with walkers and runners, or the on-road bike
lanes; faster riders should use the bike lanes. App e-bikes are not allowed on Bayshore. If
Hillsborough County adopts its proposed ordinance, the sidewalk limit would be 10 mph.

### Are there e-bike speed limits in Tampa?

City of Tampa trails have a 15 mph limit, and posted signs can set lower ones. Hillsborough County
has proposed, but not adopted, limits of 10 mph on sidewalks, 15 mph on shared paths and trails and
28 mph on roads and bike lanes, with a public hearing on November 10, 2026.
